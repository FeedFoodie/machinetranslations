Early the next morning, Ilmok and the Gaochang County Branch Manager finished their farewells. They had grown closer during the reception.

"Well then, please take care, Young Lord."

"Hahaha. Thank you for your hard work, Branch Manager," Ilmok said. "A new official notice about the school will probably arrive in a few days, so please hang in there."

"Of, of course. Hahaha!"

"Hiyah!"

Gwan Muyeol spurred his horse. The carriage departed Gaochang County and headed for Hejing County.

The Branch Manager of Gaochang County watched the carriage leave from the village entrance.

*...I feel like I forgot to tell him something.*

He had been flustered from dealing with Ilmok's threat-that-wasn't-a-threat. Soon, he realized what he had forgotten and shrugged slightly.

*Hmm. Since they are the disciple of the Exalted One and the bodyguards he assigned, there shouldn't be any major problems.*

{sep}

After a pleasant reception in Gaochang County, I traveled leisurely by carriage, a welcome change from two days ago. The bodyguard martial artists, however, did not want to see me resting.

"Young Lord. It's time to train your movement arts."

"Aigoo, dear me," I groaned. "The muscles I strained yesterday haven't healed yet. My whole body is aching."

"I don't think it was that severe."

"Actually, I once severely injured my muscles stopping Eldest Senior Brother's rampage, and I think it's acting up again," I said with a laugh. "I thought someone was supposed to protect me, but that didn't go so well."

"......"

The bodyguard martial artists fell silent when I mentioned how the rampaging Branch Manager nearly killed me yesterday. Thanks to that, I traveled comfortably in the carriage, half-dozing for some time.

Bodyguard martial artist Jang Hoe's call woke me. "Young Lord."

Before I could answer with a sigh, Gwan Muyeol spoke.

"You must not come out of the carriage."

"???"

"Horseback bandits are approaching."

"!?"

Bewildered, I pushed aside the carriage curtain and looked outside. Just as Gwan Muyeol had said, a group kicking up a sandstorm was visible in the distance.

The situation was utterly absurd.

*In Xinjiang, bandits are targeting a disciple of the Heavenly Demon?*

This was a case of madmen picking a fight with even crazier madmen. Before long, I realized why this had happened.

*That's right. We're hiding our identities right now, aren't we?*

Our carriage had no flags or emblems of the Heavenly Demon Divine Cult. We had inadvertently set a trap. As I reached this conclusion, I watched the approaching horseback bandits with a complex expression.

"They're walking right into their own deaths."

{sep}

The two bodyguard martial artists did not speed up to escape. They waited for the bandits to get right next to the carriage before attacking.

"Are you waiting for them on purpose?" I asked curiously.

Gwan Muyeol replied bluntly, "We are waiting for them to get closer."

"Is there really a need for that?"

My safety couldn't be the only reason. Even if those two went out, Jin Hayeon was still here. If my safety was the top priority, fleeing would have been the right choice. Gwan Muyeol and Jang Hoe's replies answered my question.

"Those men dared to commit evil deeds in the Divine Cult's territory," Gwan Muyeol said. "We must ensure not a single one gets away alive."

"When they get close, we'll ambush them and cut the legs of all their horses," Jang Hoe added. "Then, we plan to slaughter every last one of them."

"......"

From the moment they spotted the bandits, they were not thinking about how to deal with them, but how to kill every single one.

*Crazy.*

*Is it the influence of demonic art that gives them no aversion to killing?*

*No, martial artists in the Central Plains were similar.*

When I worked as a waiter, Mr. Taehyun often told me stories. He warned me to be careful because martial artists were terrifying people who would draw their weapons at the slightest offense.

NEIGH!!

While I was lost in thought, the horseback bandits drew closer. About twenty men rode on a dozen or so horses. Horses must have been expensive, as only two rode alone while the rest rode in pairs.

NEIGH!!

The men finally stopped, surrounding us in a circle about seven meters from the carriage. A stern-looking, middle-aged man who seemed to be the leader folded his arms and glared. A man beside him drew a broadsword and shouted.

"Hand over everything you have and be on your way! If you do, we'll spare your lives!"

A momentary silence followed the man's shout. The two bodyguard martial artists moved to slaughter the horses in a surprise attack, just as they had planned.

"Wait!" I shouted urgently, poking my head out of the carriage.

The two bodyguards, who had gripped their spear and saber to strike, looked at me with bewildered expressions. The bandits threatening us also fixed their gazes on me.

I knew if I had stayed in the carriage, the bodyguards would have quickly taken care of these bandits. They were assigned by Master, so mere bandits could not defeat them.

Still, I had a few reasons for stepping forward. The most naive reason was that I didn't want to see twenty people massacred. The more calculated reason was that killing the horses was a waste. Horses were expensive in modern times, but they were a much more important resource in this era.

Of course, inviting danger just to save a few coins was stupid, but I had another reason.

*There's no killing intent.*

These men did not feel like people threatening us for money. My experiences with Master and Eldest Senior Brother had made me sensitive to killing intent for my level, but I couldn't feel any from these men. It was also possible they were simply amateurs who couldn't emit killing intent.

*In the end, that means these guys aren't even a morning workout for the bodyguards.*

With that thought, I looked at the man in the center who appeared to be their leader.

*As I thought.*

The leader was folding his arms with a familiar expression. It was the look someone gets when forced to do something they don't want to do.

Having confirmed that much, I gave an order to the bodyguards.

"Can you subdue them without killing them, if possible?"

"Is there any need to do that?"

"There's something I want to check. Besides, isn't it a waste to kill the horses?"

My words seemed to provoke the bandits.

"These bastards must want to die!"

A few of them, their pride wounded, dismounted and charged with their weapons raised.

"......"

"We'll try our best, but if their resistance is fierce, we can't help it."

The two bodyguards sighed before launching themselves toward the men.

CLANG!!

"!?"

The bandits did not expect the bodyguards' skill. Bewilderment filled their eyes as their weapons were sliced apart in a single clash with the saber and spear.

THWACK!

The bodyguards then boldly struck the torsos of the stunned bandits. They went flying like something from a comic book. I had to wonder if they were really trying to spare them.

*They really were just trying to spare their lives.*

I watched the flying bandits with a dumbfounded expression.

"How dare you!!"

"I won't let you get away with this!!"

More bandits charged toward the two bodyguards.

"......"

They were sent flying away even faster than they had charged. The absurd spectacle continued for a moment.

"Stand back!!" the leader shouted.

He had been standing back with his arms folded. He drew a guandao, leaped from his horse, and flew straight toward Jang Hoe.

CLANG!!

"Oho," Jang Hoe muttered with interest as their weapons clashed. The leader had not been sent flying like the others.

"......"

"......"

The bandit leader and Jang Hoe continued to exchange blows, swinging their spear and guandao. Gwan Muyeol stood beside them like a warden, blocking the other bandits from interfering.

Jang Hoe, still fighting the leader, spoke as if he had realized something.

"I had a suspicion, but you bastards. You're from the military."

"!!!"

Surprise filled the bandits' faces. The bandit leader's face contorted as he shouted back.

"And you bastards are from the filthy Demonic Cult!"

The bandits' expressions hardened, finally understanding why the bodyguards were so skilled. However, someone else's expression hardened the most.

It was Jang Hoe.

"What did you just say?"

The bandit leader had just insulted their religion in front of religious fanatics.

"The entertainment is over. Now, die."

Enraged, Jang Hoe began to wield his spear in earnest. The bandit leader was instantly pushed onto the defensive.

"......"

He tenaciously blocked the spear a few times, but his guandao was finally knocked upward.

SWOOOSH!

Jang Hoe's spear thrust toward the bandit leader's heart.

"Don't kill him!" I shouted in surprise.

Jang Hoe clicked his tongue with a "Tsk" and altered his spear's path.

THWACK!

He struck the bandit leader with the shaft of his spear, then immediately subdued him by striking his acupoints.

After their leader was subdued, the bandits reacted unexpectedly.

"Hyung-nim!!"

Without hesitation, the remaining bandits charged at Jang Hoe all at once. Bandits showing loyalty was an absurd sight.

BOOM!

"Keuk..."

As a reward for their loyalty, the bandits were once again sent flying in all directions.

{sep}

The bandits knelt on the ground in a line, their acupoints sealed. I approached their leader, who wore a grim expression.

"So, why is a military man playing bandit out here?"

"......"

The man kept his mouth shut with a tragic expression, like the famously stoic Guan Yu. Fortunately, Jang Hoe answered my question instead.

"They're likely deserters, Young Lord," Jang Hoe explained. "I've heard that Xinjiang used to have many bandits, but their numbers dropped sharply after our Divine Cult settled here. Most bandits active recently are either criminals who fled the Central Plains or deserters like these men."

The remark seemed to hit a nerve. The leader's face contorted, and he shouted.

"Who are you filthy Demonic Cult bastards to insult me!"

As the leader insulted the cult again, killing intent filled the eyes of Jang Hoe and Gwan Muyeol.

"It seems we'll have to cut off that tongue of yours to teach you some manners."

As the two martial artists reached for their weapons, I intervened.

"Stop."

Simply telling them to spare the man wouldn't work.

"Just killing him is too light a punishment," I said. "Shouldn't he pay for his crimes properly?"

My words seemed to get through. The two martial artists stopped and folded their arms, looking at me as if to see what I would do next.

I turned my head to the horseback bandits.

"I'll ask again. Why are deserters playing bandit out here? If you don't answer properly, I'll cut off your heads one by one."

These men were unusually loyal for bandits, so I thought the threat might work. My prediction was correct. The leader, though grinding his teeth, began to tell his story to save his subordinates.

His story was a common one. The bandit leader was a commoner who had served in the military for over twenty years, rising to the rank of a sixth-rank Hundred Man Commander. A year ago, a man at least ten years younger became his superior, a thousand man commander. That man was from a well-known family, and the two constantly clashed. The young superior disliked the bandit leader for nagging him and set a trap, framing him for embezzling military provisions. The bandit leader was nearly executed, but these men, who had followed him for over a decade, rescued him and deserted with their horses.

After hearing the cliché story, I asked another question.

"After all that trouble, why play bandit? Selling these horses would be more than enough to start a new life."

The bandit leader responded with a furious shout.

"It's because of you! You filthy Demonic Cult bastards!"

"What nonsense is that? Because of us?"

"We came this way because we'd be hunted as deserters in the Central Plains, but no one here would take us in!" he yelled. "Every village treated us like plague demons and chased us away! If you Demonic Cult bastards didn't brainwash them, then what is it!"

Jang Hoe answered his furious shout with a cynical smile.

"Just because you have a mouth, you spew whatever you want. We merely helped the people of Xinjiang. If you hadn't tormented them in the first place, would any of this have happened?"

Jang Hoe's explanation was simple. The imperial family often sent troops to absorb Xinjiang into the Central Plains. Each time, the people of Xinjiang resisted and were massacred. During those times, demonic cultists fought alongside the residents. This apparently stopped after Master became the Heavenly Demon, but it happened frequently until a few decades ago.

Listening to this reminded me of the oppression of the Uyghurs in Xinjiang from modern times.

*It's the same now as it was back then.*

Having grasped the situation, I asked one last question.

"Well then, how many innocent people have you killed as bandits?"

The bandit leader answered with a look of wounded pride.

"I only did this so my subordinates, who risked their lives for me, wouldn't starve. We only subdued those who resisted, we never killed anyone! So stop this insult and kill me! Just spare my subordinates and kill me!"

"Hyung-nim!!"

"No, Hyung-nim! I'll die! You must survive, Hyung-nim!!"

They suddenly started acting out a scene from a historical drama.

*What a show.*

I let out a hollow laugh and opened my mouth.

"Kill you? Why would I kill you?"

These men were from the military. They practiced martial arts, not demonic art, and likely knew how to read and write.

"If you've committed a crime, you should pay the price," I said. "You don't seem to have any money, so you'll have to pay with your bodies."

The perfect manpower for my job in Gaochang County had just rolled right in.
