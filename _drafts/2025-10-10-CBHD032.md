The next morning came early.

"Please take care, Young Lord."

"Hahaha. Thank you for your hard work, Branch Manager. A new notice about the school should arrive in a few days, so please hang in there."

"Of course. Hahaha!"

Ilmok and the Gaochang County Branch Manager had grown closer during the reception. After they finished their farewells, Gwan Muyeol spurred his horse. The carriage departed Gaochang County and headed for Hejing County.

The Gaochang County Branch Manager watched the carriage leave from the village entrance.

*...I feel like I forgot to tell him something.*

The Branch Manager was flustered from dealing with Ilmok's subtle threat. He soon realized what he had forgotten and shrugged slightly.

*Hmm. Since they are the disciple of the Exalted One and the bodyguards he assigned, there shouldn't be any major problems.*

{sep}

After a pleasant reception in Gaochang County, I traveled leisurely by carriage, a stark contrast to two days ago.

"Young Lord. It's time to train your movement arts."

The bodyguard martial artists, unwilling to let me rest, spoke up.

"Aigoo, my muscles are still sore from yesterday. My whole body aches."

"I don't think it was that severe."

"Actually, I think an old injury is acting up. I hurt my muscles badly stopping Eldest Senior Brother's rampage once. Hahaha. I thought someone was supposed to protect me, but that didn't go so well, did it?"

"......"

I reminded them how the rampaging Branch Manager nearly killed me yesterday. The bodyguard martial artists fell silent. Thanks to that, I dozed comfortably in the carriage for a while.

"Young Lord."

I sighed and woke to bodyguard martial artist Jang Hoe's call. Before I could answer, Gwan Muyeol spoke.

"Don't come out of the carriage."

"???"

"Horseback bandits are approaching."

"!?"

Bewildered, I pushed aside the carriage curtain and looked outside. Just as Gwan Muyeol said, a group kicking up a sandstorm was visible in the distance. It was an utterly absurd situation.

*In Xinjiang, bandits are targeting a disciple of the Heavenly Demon?*

Madmen were picking a fight with even crazier madmen. I soon realized why this was happening.

*That's right. We're hiding our identities right now, aren't we?*

Our carriage had no flags or emblems of the Heavenly Demon Divine Cult. We had unintentionally set a trap. With this realization, I watched the approaching bandits with a complex expression.

"They're walking right into their own deaths."

{sep}

The two bodyguards did not speed up to escape. They waited until the bandits were right beside the carriage before attacking.

"Are you waiting for them on purpose?"

I asked out of curiosity. Gwan Muyeol replied bluntly.

"We're waiting for them to get closer."

"Is there a need for that?"

I couldn't say it was for my protection. Even if those two left, Jin Hayeon was still here. Besides, if my safety was the priority, we should have fled immediately.

Gwan Muyeol and Jang Hoe's replies answered my question.

"Those men dared to commit evil in the Divine Cult's territory. We'll make sure not a single one gets away alive."

"When they get close, we'll ambush them and cut their horses' legs. Then we'll slaughter every last one, so they can't escape."

"......"

This meant they weren't thinking about how to deal with the bandits. They were thinking about how to kill every single one without letting any escape.

*Crazy.*

*Is it the influence of demonic art that makes them so comfortable with killing?*

*No, martial artists in the Central Plains were similar.*

When I was a waiter, Mr. Taehyun often told me this. He warned me to be careful because martial artists were terrifying people who drew their weapons at the slightest offense.

NEIGH!!

While I was lost in thought, the bandits drew closer. About twenty men rode a dozen or so horses. Horses must be expensive. Only two rode alone while the rest rode in pairs.

NEIGH!!

The men finally stopped. They surrounded us in a circle about seven meters from the carriage. A stern, middle-aged man who looked like the leader folded his arms and glared at us. A man beside him drew a broadsword and shouted.

"Hand over everything you have and go! If you do, we'll spare your lives!"

A brief silence followed his shout. The two bodyguards were about to move and slaughter the horses as planned.

"Wait!"

I shouted urgently and poked my head out of the carriage.

The two bodyguards gripped their spear and saber, ready to strike. They looked at me, bewildered. The threatening bandits also fixed their gazes on me.

I knew it too. If I had stayed in the carriage, the bodyguards would have quickly taken care of these bandits. Master assigned them, so there was no way mere bandits could defeat them. Still, I stepped forward for a few reasons.

The most naive reason was that I didn't want to see twenty people massacred.

The calculated reason was that killing the horses was a waste. Horses were expensive in modern times, but they were a much more important and versatile resource in this era.

Of course, inviting danger to save a few coins was stupid, but I had another reason.

*There's no killing intent.*

They didn't feel like men threatening us for money. Perhaps my experiences with Master or Eldest Senior Brother made me sensitive to killing intent for my level, but I felt no killing intent from these men. Or maybe they were just amateurs who couldn't even project it.

*In the end, this means these guys aren't even a morning workout for the bodyguards.*

With that thought, I looked at the man in the center who seemed to be their leader.

*As I thought.*

The leader folded his arms with a familiar expression. It was the look someone gets when forced to do something they don't want to do.

Having confirmed this, I ordered the bodyguards who were ready to strike.

"Can you subdue them without killing them?"

"Is there any need for that?"

"There's something I want to check. Besides, isn't it a waste to kill the horses?"

My words seemed to provoke the bandits.

"These bastards must want to die!"

A few of them, their pride wounded, dismounted and charged with weapons raised.

"......"

"We'll try our best, but if they resist fiercely, we can't help it."

The two bodyguards sighed, then launched themselves toward the men with their movement arts.

CLANG!!

"!?"

They clearly didn't expect the bodyguards' skill. Bewilderment filled the bandits' eyes as the bodyguards' saber and spear sliced their weapons apart in a single clash.

THWACK!

The bodyguards then struck the stunned bandits' torsos. I watched the bandits fly away like something from a comic book. I wondered if they were really trying to spare them.

*They really were just trying to spare their lives.*

I watched the flying bandits, dumbfounded.

"How dare you!!"

"I won't let you get away with this!"

More bandits charged the two bodyguards.

"......"

They flew away even faster than they had charged. The absurd spectacle continued for a moment.

"Stand back!!"

The leader, who had been standing back with folded arms, shouted. He drew a guandao, leaped from his horse, and flew straight toward Jang Hoe.

CLANG!!

"......"

Jang Hoe clashed his spear with the leader's weapon and muttered with interest. Unlike the others, the leader hadn't been sent flying from a single clash.

"......"

"......"

The bandit leader and Jang Hoe continued to exchange blows, swinging their spear and guandao. Gwan Muyeol stood nearby like a warden, blocking the other bandits from interfering.

Just then, Jang Hoe spoke as if he realized something.

"I had a suspicion. You bastards are from the military."

"!!!"

Surprise filled the bandits' faces. The bandit leader's face contorted as he shouted.

"And you bastards are from the filthy Demonic Cult!"

The bandits' expressions hardened. They finally seemed to understand why the bodyguards were so skilled. However, Jang Hoe's expression hardened the most. The bandit leader had dared to insult his religion.

"What did you just say?"

"The entertainment is over. Now, die."

Enraged, Jang Hoe began to wield his spear in earnest. The bandit leader was instantly pushed onto the defensive.

"......"

He tenaciously blocked the spear a few times, but his guandao finally gave way and was knocked upward.

SWOOOSH!

In that moment, Jang Hoe's spear thrust toward the bandit leader's heart.

"Don't kill him!"

I shouted in surprise. Jang Hoe clicked his tongue, "Tsk," and altered his spear's path.

THWACK!

Jang Hoe struck the bandit leader with the spear's shaft, not the blade. He then immediately subdued him by striking his acupoints.

After their leader was subdued, the bandits reacted unexpectedly.

"Hyung-nim!!"

Without hesitation, the remaining bandits all charged at Jang Hoe. Loyal bandits. It was an absurd sight.

BOOM!

"Keuk..."

For their loyalty, the bandits were once again sent flying.

{sep}

The bandits knelt in a line, their acupoints sealed. I approached their leader, who wore a grim expression.

"So, why is a military man playing bandit out here?"

"......"

The man kept his mouth shut, his tragic expression reminiscent of the famously honorable Guan Yu. Fortunately, Jang Hoe answered for him.

"They're likely deserters, Young Lord. I've heard that Xinjiang used to have many bandits, but their numbers dropped sharply after our Divine Cult settled here. Most bandits active now are either criminals who fled the Central Plains after committing grave sins, or they're deserters like these men."

The remark hit a nerve. The leader's face contorted, and he shouted.

"Who are you filthy Demonic Cult bastards to insult me!"

The leader insulted the cult again. Killing intent filled the eyes of Jang Hoe and Gwan Muyeol.

"It seems we'll have to cut off that tongue of yours to teach you some manners."

As the two martial artists reached for their weapons, I intervened.

"Stop."

But simply telling them to spare the man unconditionally wouldn't work.

"Just killing him is too light a punishment. Shouldn't he pay for his crimes properly?"

My words seemed to get through. The two martial artists stopped, then folded their arms and looked at me. They seemed to be waiting to see what I would do. I turned my head and asked the bandits.

"I'll ask again. Why are deserters playing bandit out here? If you don't answer properly, I'll cut off your heads one by one."

These men were unusually loyal for bandits, so I thought the threat might work. My prediction was correct. The leader ground his teeth but began to tell his story, perhaps to save his subordinates.

His story was a common one.

The bandit leader was a commoner who had served in the military for over twenty years, rising to the rank of a sixth-rank Hundred Man Commander. A year ago, a man at least ten years younger became his superior, a thousand man commander. That man was from a well-known family, and the two of them were constantly in conflict.

The young man disliked the bandit leader for nagging him and ended up setting a trap. He framed him for embezzling military provisions. The bandit leader was about to be executed. These bandits, who had followed him for over a decade, rescued him one night and deserted with their horses.

After hearing the cliché story, I asked again.

"After all that trouble to escape, why are you playing bandit? Frankly, selling these horses would be more than enough to start a new life."

The bandit leader responded with a furious shout.

"It's because of you! You filthy Demonic Cult bastards!"

"What nonsense is that? Because of us?"

"We came here because we'd be hunted as deserters in the Central Plains, but no one would take us in! Every village we visited looked at us like we were plague demons and chased us away! If you Demonic Cult bastards didn't brainwash them, then what is it!"

Jang Hoe answered his furious shout with a cynical smile.

"You spew whatever you want just because you have a mouth. We only helped the people of Xinjiang. If you hadn't tormented them in the first place, would any of this have happened?"

Jang Hoe's explanation was simple.

The imperial family often sent troops to absorb Xinjiang into the Central Plains. Each time, the people of Xinjiang resisted and were massacred. He explained that during those times, demonic cultists fought alongside the Xinjiang residents. It apparently stopped after Master became the Heavenly Demon, but it happened frequently until a few decades ago.

Listening to this reminded me of the oppression of the Uyghurs in Xinjiang from modern times.

*It's the same now as it was back then.*

Having grasped the situation, I asked one last question.

"Well then, how many innocent people have you killed as bandits?"

The bandit leader answered with a look of wounded pride.

"I only did this so my subordinates, who risked their lives for me, wouldn't starve. Even when people resisted, we only subdued them. We never killed anyone! So stop insulting me and just kill me! Spare my subordinates, just kill me!"

"Hyung-nim!!"

"No, Hyung-nim! I'll die! You must survive, Hyung-nim!"

Suddenly, they started acting out a scene from a historical drama.

*What a show.*

I laughed hollowly in disbelief and spoke.

"Kill you? Why would I kill you?"

These men are from the military. They practice martial arts, not ‘demonic art’. Since they've learned martial arts, they should know how to read and write to some extent.

"If you've committed a crime, you should pay the price. You don't seem to have any money, so you'll have to pay with your bodies."

This meant the perfect manpower for my plans in Gaochang County had just rolled right in.
