Chapter 32: Personnel Recruitment (1)

Early the next morning.

"Well then, please take care, Young Lord."

"Hahaha. Thank you for your hard work, Branch Manager. A new official notice about the school will probably arrive in a few days, so please hang in there until then."

"Of, of course. Hahaha!"

After Ilmok and the Gaochang County Branch Manager, who had grown closer through the reception, finished their farewells.

"Hiyah!"

Gwan Muyeol spurred his horse, and the carriage departed Gaochang County, heading for Hejing County.

The Branch Manager of Gaochang County, watching the carriage leave from the village entrance, thought to himself.

*...I feel like I forgot to tell him something.*

The Branch Manager had been so flustered from dealing with Ilmok's threat-that-wasn't-a-threat.

And soon, realizing what he had forgotten, the Branch Manager gave a slight shrug.

*Hmm. Since they are the disciple of the Exalted One and the bodyguards he assigned, there shouldn't be any major problems.*

★★★

Having received a pleasant reception in Gaochang County, I was traveling leisurely by carriage, unlike two days ago.

"Young Lord. It is now time to train your movement arts."

The bodyguard martial artists, who didn't want to see me resting, opened their mouths to say so.

"Aigoo, dear me. The muscles I strained yesterday haven't healed yet, so my whole body is aching."

"I don't think it was that severe."

"Actually, I once severely injured my muscles while stopping Eldest Senior Brother's rampage, and I think it's acting up again. Hahaha. I thought someone was supposed to protect me, but it seems that didn't go so well."

"......"

When I brought up how I was nearly killed by the rampaging Branch Manager yesterday, the bodyguard martial artists were struck dumb.

Thanks to that, I traveled comfortably in the carriage, half-dozing, for some time.

"Young Lord."

Before I, who had woken up with a sigh at bodyguard martial artist Jang Hoe's call, could answer, Gwan Muyeol's voice was heard this time.

"You must not come out of the carriage."

"???"

"Horseback bandits are approaching."

"!?"

With a bewildered expression, I pushed aside the carriage curtain and looked outside.

Indeed, just as Gwan Muyeol had said, a group kicking up a sandstorm was visible in the distance.

It was an utterly absurd situation.

*In Xinjiang, bandits are targeting a disciple of the Heavenly Demon?*

It was a case of madmen picking a fight with even crazier madmen.

And before long, I was able to realize why this situation had unfolded.

*That's right. We're hiding our identities right now, aren't we?*

The carriage we were currently riding in had no flags or emblems related to the Heavenly Demon Divine Cult.

It wasn't intentional, but we had inadvertently set a trap.

My thoughts having reached that point, I watched the approaching horseback bandits with a complex and subtle expression.

"They're walking right into their own deaths."

★★★

For some reason, the two bodyguard martial artists neither sped up the carriage to escape nor attacked the bandits until they were right next to the carriage.

"Are you waiting for the bandits on purpose?"

When I asked this out of curiosity, Gwan Muyeol replied in a blunt tone.

"We are waiting for them to get closer."

"Is there really a need for that?"

It was hard to say it was because of my bodyguard. Even if those two went out, Jin Hayeon would still be here. Besides, if my safety was the top priority, fleeing in the first place would have been the right thing to do.

And my question was answered thanks to Gwan Muyeol and Jang Hoe's replies.

"Those men have dared to commit evil deeds in the Divine Cult's territory, so this is to ensure not a single one of them gets away alive."

"When they get close. We will ambush them and cut the legs of all their horses. Then, we plan to slaughter every last one of them, leaving them unable to escape."

"......"

This meant that from the moment they spotted the horseback bandits, they weren't thinking about how to deal with them, but how to kill every single one without letting any escape.

*Crazy.*

Is it because of the influence of demonic art and heretic doctrine that they have no aversion to killing?

*No, martial artists in the Central Plains were similar.*

Back when I worked as a waiter, I often heard it from Mr. Taehyun. He warned me to be careful because martial artists were terrifying people who would draw their weapons at the slightest offense.

NEIGH!!

Even as I was lost in such thoughts, the horseback bandits were drawing closer by the second.

About twenty men were mounted on a dozen or so horses.

Perhaps because horses are expensive, only two of them were riding alone, while the rest were riding in pairs.

NEIGH!!

The men finally stopped, surrounding us in a circle about two zhang away from the carriage.

A stern-looking, middle-aged man who appeared to be the leader simply folded his arms and glared at us, while a man who had stopped beside him drew a broadsword and shouted.

"Hand over everything you have and be on your way! If you do, we'll spare your lives!"

A momentary silence followed the man's shout.

And just as the two bodyguard martial artists were about to move to slaughter the horses in a surprise attack as they'd said.

"Wait!"

I shouted urgently, poking my head out of the carriage entrance.

The two bodyguard martial artists, who had gripped their spear and saber to strike, looked at me with bewildered expressions.

Furthermore, the gazes of the bandits who were threatening us also fixed on me.

I knew it too. If I had just stayed put in the carriage, the bodyguard martial artists would have soon taken care of these bandits.

They were bodyguard martial artists assigned by Master, so there was no way they would be defeated by mere bandits.

Nevertheless, there were a few reasons I stepped forward.

The most naive and foolish reason was that I didn't want to see twenty people get massacred.

And as for the calculated reason, it was a waste to kill the horses. Horses were expensive even in modern times, but in this era, they were a much more important resource, used for a far wider variety of purposes.

Of course, inviting danger just to save a few coins was a stupid thing to do, but there was a reason for this too.

*There's no killing intent.*

It was unlike men who were threatening us for money.

Perhaps because of my experiences dealing with Master or Eldest Senior Brother, I was quite sensitive to killing intent for my level.

However, I couldn't feel any killing intent from these men. On the other hand, it could be that they were such amateurs they couldn't even emit killing intent.

*Then in the end, it means these guys aren't even a morning workout for the bodyguard martial artists.*

With that thought, I looked at the man in the center of the group who appeared to be their leader.

*As I thought.*

The leader was folding his arms with an expression that was quite familiar to me.

It was an expression anyone would make often. That specific look one gets when forced to do something they don't want to do.

Having confirmed that much, I gave an order to the bodyguard martial artists who were ready to swing their weapons.

"Can you subdue them without killing them if possible?"

"Is there any need to do so?"

"There's something I want to check. And besides, isn't it a waste to kill the horses?"

Did my words provoke the bandits?

"These bastards must want to die!"

A few of them, their pride seemingly wounded, dismounted and charged with their weapons raised.

"Hoo."

"We will try our best, but if their resistance is fierce, we can't help it."

And the two bodyguard martial artists let out a sigh before launching themselves toward the men using their movement arts.

CLANG!!

"!?"

Did they not expect the skill of the two bodyguard martial artists?

Bewilderment filled the eyes of the bandits whose weapons were sliced apart in a single clash with the saber and spear wielded by the bodyguard martial artists.

THWACK!

Then, the bodyguard martial artists boldly struck the torsos of the stunned bandits.

Watching the bandits who were hit go flying like a scene from a comic book, I had to wonder if they were really trying to spare them.

*They really were just trying to spare their lives.*

While I watched the flying bandits with a dumbfounded expression.

"How dare you!!"

"I won't let you get away with this!!"

More bandits charged toward the two bodyguard martial artists.

BOOM!!

And they were sent flying away at a speed even faster than they had charged.

That absurd spectacle continued for a moment.

"Stand back!!"

The leader, who had been standing back with his arms folded, shouted this, then drew a guandao and leaped from his horse, flying straight toward Jang Hoe.

CLANG!!

"Oho."

Jang Hoe, who had clashed his spear with the bandit leader's weapon, muttered with interest. It seemed to be because, although it was just one clash, the leader hadn't been sent flying like the other bandits.

CLANG!

CLANG!

After that, the bandit leader and Jang Hoe continued to exchange blows, swinging their spear and guandao.

Gwan Muyeol stood beside them like a warden, blocking the other bandits from interfering.

Just then, Jang Hoe, who was fighting the leader, spoke as if he had realized something.

"I had a suspicion, but you bastards. You're from the military."

"!!!"

Surprise filled the bandits' faces at Jang Hoe's words, and the bandit leader's face contorted as he shouted.

"And you bastards are from the filthy the Demonic Cult!"

The bandits' expressions hardened, as if they finally understood why the bodyguard martial artists were so skilled.

However, there was someone else whose expression hardened the most.

"What did you just say?"

It was the bandit leader who had dared to insult their religion in front of heretic religious fanatics.

"The entertainment is over. Now, die."

As the enraged Jang Hoe began to wield his spear in earnest, the bandit leader was instantly pushed onto the defensive.

CLANG!

He tenaciously blocked the spear a few times, but in the end, the bandit leader's guandao, unable to hold out, was knocked upward.

SWOOOSH!

In that moment, Jang Hoe's spear thrust toward the bandit leader's heart.

"Don't kill him!"

When I shouted in surprise, Jang Hoe clicked his tongue with a "Tsk," and altered the path of his spear.

THWACK!

Jang Hoe struck the bandit leader with the shaft of his spear instead of the blade, then immediately approached and subdued him by striking his acupoints.

And right after their leader was subdued, the bandits showed an unexpected reaction.

"Hyung-nim!!"

Without a moment's hesitation, the bandits who were still standing all charged at Jang Hoe at once.

Bandits showing loyalty. It was an utterly absurd sight.

BOOM!

"Keuk..."

As a reward for their loyalty, the bandits were once again sent flying in all directions.

★★★

The bandits, their acupoints sealed, were kneeling on the ground in a line.

I approached the leader among them, who wore a grim expression.

"So, why is a man from the military playing bandit out here?"

"......"

The man kept his mouth shut with a tragic expression, as if he were possessed by the spirit of Guan Yu, who was known for his integrity.

And fortunately, Jang Hoe answered my question in his stead.

"They are likely deserters, Young Lord. I've heard that in the old days, there were many bandits and mountain bandits from Xinjiang, but their numbers have dropped sharply since our the Divine Cult settled here. Most of the bandits and mountain bandits active recently are either criminals who committed grave sins in the Central Plains and fled, or deserters like these men."

As if the remark hit a nerve, the leader's face contorted, and he shouted.

"Who are you filthy the Demonic Cult bastards to insult me!"

As the leader insulted the cult once again, killing intent filled the eyes of Jang Hoe and Gwan Muyeol.

"It seems we'll have to cut off that tongue of yours to teach you some manners."

As the two martial artists were about to draw their weapons, I intervened.

"Stop."

However, simply telling them to spare this man unconditionally wouldn't work.

"Just killing him is too light a punishment. Shouldn't he pay for his crimes properly?"

As if my words got through, the two martial artists who were about to draw their weapons stopped, then folded their arms and looked at me. It was as if they were going to wait and see what I would do.

And I turned my head to ask the horseback bandits.

"I'll ask again. Why are deserters playing bandit out here? If you don't answer properly, I'll cut off your heads one by one."

Since these men were unusually loyal for bandits, I made the threat, thinking it might just work.

And my prediction was correct.

The leader, though grinding his teeth, began to recount his story, perhaps to save his subordinates.

Once I heard the story, it turned out to be a common one.

The bandit leader was a man who had started as a commoner and served in the military for over twenty years, rising to the rank of a sixth-rank Hundred Man Commander.

Then, a year ago, a man at least ten years younger than him came in as his superior, a thousand man commander.

Naturally, that man was from a fairly well-known family, and the two of them were constantly in conflict.

And that young man, who disliked the bandit leader for nagging him about every little thing, ended up setting a trap for him.

He framed him for embezzling military provisions.

The bandit leader was on the verge of execution, and these bandits here, who had followed him for over a decade, moved secretly at night to rescue him and deserted with their horses.

After hearing the whole, rather cliché story, I asked again.

"After going through all that trouble to escape, why are you playing bandit? Frankly, just selling the horses you have here would be more than enough to start a new life."

In response to my question, the bandit leader let out a furious shout.

"It's because of you!! You filthy the Demonic Cult bastards!!"

"What kind of nonsense is that? Because of us?"

"We came this way because it was obvious we'd be hunted as deserters if we returned to the Central Plains, but no one would take us in! Every village we went to looked at us like we were plague demons and was busy chasing us away! If this isn't because you the Demonic Cult bastards brainwashed them, then what is it!"

The one who answered his furious shout was Jang Hoe, who wore a cynical smile.

"Just because you have a mouth, you spew whatever you want. We merely helped the people of Xinjiang. If you hadn't tormented the people of Xinjiang in the first place, would any of this have happened?"

Jang Hoe's subsequent explanation was simple.

The imperial family often dispatched troops to absorb the Xinjiang region as part of the Central Plains, and each time, the people of Xinjiang resisted, getting massacred in the process. He explained that during that time, demonic cultists fought alongside the residents of Xinjiang.

It had apparently stopped after Master ascended to the position of the Heavenly Demon, but it was something that happened frequently until a few decades ago.

Somehow, listening to this reminded me of the oppression of the Uyghurs in Xinjiang that I often heard about in modern times.

*It's the same now as it was back then.*

Having grasped the situation, I asked one last question.

"Well then, how many innocent people have you killed while playing bandit until now?"

The bandit leader answered my question with a look of wounded pride.

"I only did this because I couldn't let my subordinates, who risked their lives to save me, starve to death. Even those who resisted, we only subdued them, we never killed them! So stop this insult and kill me! Spare my subordinates, and just kill me!"

"Hyung-nim!!"

"No, Hyung-nim! I'll die! You must survive, Hyung-nim!!"

Suddenly, these guys started acting out a scene straight out of a historical drama.

*What a show.*

I let out a hollow laugh in disbelief and opened my mouth.

"Kill you? Why would I kill you?"

These men are from the military. Moreover, they practice martial arts, not ‘demonic art’, and since they've learned martial arts, they should know how to read and write to some extent.

"If you've committed a crime, shouldn't you pay the price? You don't seem to have any money, so you'll have to pay with your bodies."

This meant that the perfect manpower for the job I had planned in Gaochang County had just rolled right in.
