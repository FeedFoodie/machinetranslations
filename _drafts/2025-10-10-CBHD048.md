"Researching food and medicinal herbs, and training physicians?" Master asked, summarizing my words.

I nodded in response. "That's right."

"I can somewhat understand researching food and herbs," Master said. "But does counseling also require research?"

"What about when Eldest Senior Brother had his enlightenment, or what happened with Third Senior Brother?" I countered.

"Are you talking about the conversations you had with Jinhak and Wanpyeong?"

"Yes. I had completely different conversations with each of them. Even if it's all counseling, I think the approach should change depending on the symptoms of the side effects."

"Hmm… I think I understand what you mean."

People often lumped it all together as mental illness, but the symptoms varied widely. The counseling method needed to adapt to the person and their symptoms.

"Without even considering our demonic art's side effects, what about the condition common folk call 'infection'?" I continued. "Isn't that an illness that erupts from suppressing anger and frustration? Just talking about one's hardships or anger can make it easier to manage those negative emotions."

I had actually heard this from Senior Official Lee Jiyeon, the one who helped me remember coffee.

She was once diagnosed with depression because of a difficult petitioner, but after completely mastering the black flames, she became a fighter who would confront anyone who caused trouble, just like how she argued with the petitioner who stabbed me that day.

She said that after mastering the black flames, she realized a truth thanks to counseling with a psychiatrist. Enduring it all alone only hurts you. You have to fight back against those who hurt you to keep resentment from building up.

There was also the folktale about the king with donkey ears. People risked developing mental illness just by bottling things up, and that illness could progress to the point of destroying the body.

Of course, I couldn't mention that folktale out loud, as there was no need to arouse unnecessary suspicion.

In any case, the important thing here was the act of 'speaking' itself.

"People feel much more at ease just by telling someone about the problems that torment them," I explained. "The issue is when the problem is something you can't easily tell just anyone."

It was just like the folktale about the king with donkey ears.

"That's why the new physicians we train must also serve as outlets for secrets. A safe outlet that won't spread my secrets elsewhere."

Unlike the bamboo forest in the folktale, we needed people who would protect secrets.

"Based on the records of counseling sessions and patient stories, the physicians will improve their methods and treatment plans. Access to that information must be restricted to only the physicians of the new clinic and the Cult Leader."

Master listened to my explanation in silence, then frowned. "Ilmok, as you said, the term 'infection' does exist among the common folk. For them, a place to talk or share secrets might be necessary, but it doesn't seem right for the martial artists of our Divine Cult. Relying on medicine because you can't overcome side effects… I'm concerned it will make the Divine Cult's martial artists weak."

It was Master's stubbornly old-fashioned conclusion that one must overcome it with mental strength.

After briefly wondering how to respond, I decided to take a strong stance.

"…After maintaining the traditional methods you speak of, Master, what has our Divine Cult accomplished? Weren't we driven out of the Central Plains two hundred years ago, and haven't we been hiding here ever since?"

The moment my words ended, a terrifying demonic qi erupted from Master.

It was completely different from the duel earlier. There was no forced killing intent, but the pressure was incomparably dense.

"What did you just say?"

Even without any killing intent, the pressure of his aura was so thick it was hard to breathe.

My mind and body were already exhausted from the duel, and I felt like I could pass out at any moment, but I couldn't stop talking.

"If nothing changes with the methods we've used for two hundred years, there's only one solution. We must create change, Master."

"So, are you saying this change you speak of will revive our Divine Cult?"

"I can't guarantee a revival, but if we have more medicine and physicians to help with side effects, the number of martial artists will increase. Wouldn't that be a great help in returning to the Central Plains?"

"In martial arts, quality is more important than quantity. One peerless master who has overcome the side effects is more important than a hundred weak martial artists who couldn't."

"But isn't it common sense that the chance of a peerless master emerging from a thousand martial artists is higher than from a hundred?"

At my last words, Master gave no reply, simply continuing to pressure me with his murderous aura.

Even as my head spun and I felt I would faint, I gritted my teeth and endured Master's pressure and gaze.

Then, just as I was on the verge of collapsing, the pressure suddenly vanished like a spring breeze.

"I understand your admirable desire for the Divine Cult's advancement, and you're right, there can be no progress if we only follow the past."

Right after that terrifying demonic qi that tormented me disappeared, I almost let out a hollow laugh.

*Admirable desire for the Divine Cult, my ass.*

This idea didn't come from pitying those suffering from side effects, nor from a desire for the Divine Cult's advancement.

There was only one major reason I thought of a Heavenly Demon Divine Cult mental ward. It was for my own mental health.

*I saw with my own two eyes what happened to Gwan Muyeol, whose side effects are so similar to mine. How can I just ignore that?*

I didn't want to live a life of grinding up poisonous mushrooms, mixing them into a drink, and then staggering around muttering about unidentified aliens.

That worry grew even larger after my realm suddenly rose again because of Jang Hoe's surprise attack. I was worried that I might really be consumed by the side effects and go insane.

So, the Heavenly Demon Divine Cult mental ward plan was insurance for my future. I realized the need to cultivate physicians who could treat me and make medicine for me, just in case my side effects became unbearably strong.

Unaware of my true intentions, Master made an absurd proposal.

"In that case, how about you take charge of this new clinic? You have experience alleviating Jinhak's and Wanpyeong's side effects through counseling, and with the black beans, you seem to have some knowledge of food and herbs. You seem perfect for it."

"My being able to help the two Senior Brothers was just a one-in-ten-thousand coincidence. Besides, my knowledge is limited to a few teas and foods, including black beans. I know next to nothing about medicinal herbs."

*This old man is saying something terrifying.*

I only knew a few things because I was from the modern world. I was a civil servant, not a psychiatrist.

I was a layman in psychiatry, which was precisely why I was arguing for the creation and development of a mental ward. I had none of the knowledge needed to cure my future self.

However, since I had studied public administration, this kind of work suited me better. I couldn't become a physician, but I could build a system to train them.

"First, we need to select a few physicians from the clinic to research herbs and counseling, and start building a system. I'll tell them about my conversations with Eldest and Third Senior Brothers, and share what I know about teas and foods. Furthermore, we should collect medical texts and folk remedies from the Central Plains and the Outer Regions, and use them to find and test herbs and foods that calm the mind and body."

"By testing, do you mean feeding those herbs and foods to someone?"

"If we start with small amounts and carefully observe the progress, there shouldn't be any major problems. The tests will be based on medical texts and folk remedies anyway, so it's unlikely a small amount would cause serious issues. Besides, we have Gwan Muyeol's case, don't we?"

Gwan Muyeol was secretly using poisonous mushrooms as a treatment.

From the perspective of someone planning a mental ward, he could be considered a great success story.

"Hmm. It might not be a bad idea to reassign Gwan Muyeol from the Guardian Hall to this new clinic and have him receive 'various' treatments."

"Th-that also seems like a good plan."

In an instant, Gwan Muyeol had become a guinea pig.

I felt a little sorry for him, but I also thought it was a relief. I didn't mean I was relieved he'd become a guinea pig for my sake. I'm not that much of a scumbag.

*He's already almost consumed by the side effects. Prioritizing treatment over training or duties will be better for Martial Artist Gwan. Yes.*

Having quickly rationalized it, I swiftly changed the subject.

"And for training additional physicians, how about we cultivate them from among those currently being educated across Xinjiang?"

"Hmm. Weren't we planning to raise the talented children as martial artists? They barely have enough time to learn martial arts. There won't be nearly enough time to also teach them about herbs, food, and counseling. Besides, what if they succumb to the demonic art's side effects? Wouldn't that create an even bigger problem?"

If a psychiatrist suffered from mental illness, there's no telling what disaster could befall their patients. From that perspective, Master's point was valid.

But the 'children' I was talking about weren't those children.

"In the two-year basic education course, there will be children who are smart but lack talent in martial arts."

"!!! So you mean we raise the children with martial arts talent as martial artists, and the smart ones as physicians!"

"We don't necessarily have to raise them all as physicians. It would be good to divide them into two groups, one learning medicine and the other learning administrative work."

Master, who had been listening with an impressed expression, soon frowned. "If we entrust them with administrative work too, won't that weaken the position of the martial artists who practice demonic arts? Our Heavenly Demon Divine Cult is a group that reveres 'martial prowess' according to its doctrine. In the end, martial skill is necessary to punish the wicked."

My mind raced at Master's point and quickly produced an answer.

"Won't the heads of each hall, division, and pavilion be held by those who have reached the Transcendent Demon Realm, just as they are now? The masters will hold the leadership positions, and the others will simply be in charge of practical affairs below them."

Even after that, Master and I endlessly discussed the initial plans for the mental ward.

I had some ideas prepared beforehand, but my mind also worked without pause in response to Master's immediate questions.

It wasn't because I was a genius.

—Not enough!! Think of a more concrete plan!
—So? What's the contingency plan if something goes wrong?

It was because the side effects, which had subsided a bit thanks to my arduous training, came rushing back like a flood.

*The tables have turned. Damn it.*

I had thought of the mental ward to prepare for the possibility of going mad from the side effects, but thanks to those same side effects, a more systematic plan for the ward was being created.
