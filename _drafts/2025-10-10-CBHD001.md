Work-life balance was a life philosophy that was once trendy, along with YOLO. Lately, people treated YOLO as completely outdated, but 'work-life balance' was still a term used from time to time. I had considered work-life balance my highest priority when choosing a career.

I had no intention of becoming incredibly rich. Honestly, it didn't seem possible to get rich by working like a madman, whether at a large corporation or anywhere else. If I could just earn enough to take care of myself, I wanted to live a quiet life enjoying my hobbies.

If I had to work 80 hours a week to earn eight million won a month, I would rather earn around three million a month working only 40 hours a week.

In the early to mid-2010s, when I entered the job market, the symbol of work-life balance was undoubtedly being a civil servant. Unlike in private companies, I wouldn't have to worry about being fired or the company going bankrupt. Civil servants were thought to leave work right at 6 PM every day, unlike regular employees worn down by corporate power trips and overtime.

So, in a word.

"Who would've known it'd be like this? Fuck..."

I was deceived by a foolish delusion. Civil servants leaving work on the dot? That was utter nonsense. It might have been possible in the old days, but not anymore.

You think it's uncomfortable to be constantly anxious about getting fired from a private company, always watching your boss's mood? Back then, I didn't know that while I couldn't be fired, that bastard couldn't be fired either.

Of course, if I voiced such complaints these days, the response I would get was obvious.

> "So what? Did someone coerce you and force you to become a civil servant?"

Right. No one coerced me. I crawled in here on my own two feet because I wanted to.

However, even so. If I had known I was going to die like that, I wouldn't have become a civil servant.

Where did it all go wrong? Was it when I prepared for the civil service exam, captivated by a foolish delusion? Was it when I decided the 9th-grade salary seemed too low, so I invested more time to aim for the 7th-grade position?

Was it that because I'd spent three years studying to become a 7th-grade civil servant, I couldn't bring myself to quit and just kept trudging along, and before I knew it, five years had passed?

"When do I get to go home today…? Haah."

As I muttered the complaint to myself, a familiar noise pierced my ears.

"Hey, you can't come in here!"

"If you keep this up, I'm calling the police!"

"If you can't do it, bring your superior!! Your superior!! Before I kill you all, you fucking bitches!!"

It was clearly a problem with a citizen complaint. I turned my head toward the noise without much thought and saw an old man trying to force his way inside while arguing with people.

Just my luck, the moment I turned my head, my eyes met Senior Official Lee Jiyeon’s. She was arguing with the citizen.

"Senior Official Seo Jihun!"

At her shout, the citizen's gaze naturally turned toward me.

*Ha. The aggro's shifted to me.*

The moment our eyes met, I knew trying to avoid it would only make the situation worse. Still, I turned my head aside, clinging to a final sliver of hope.

*Wow. Fuck. Is this the difference in experience?*

That bastard, my Team Leader, had already vacated his seat and escaped.

"Are you the head honcho here?" the fiercely glaring old man asked.

I sighed inwardly and approached him. "I'm not the head honcho. First, please calm down and tell me what's going on. What brings you here?"

In response, the old man screamed at the top of his lungs. More than ninety percent of his sentences were profanity, making it hard to understand him.

As if to help, Senior Official Lee Jiyeon briefly explained the situation. "An official notice came down, and the restrictions for basic welfare recipients changed, right? It's because of that issue..."

"......"

It seemed he had come to protest being removed from the list of basic welfare recipients.

There was once a trend in movies, dramas, and novels. The rich are evil, and the poor are good. However, reality was not like that. As the saying goes, generosity comes from a full granary. The anger and tantrums of those with no leeway left in their lives were beyond imagination.

"You goddamn bastards! How much have I done for this country, huh? You fucking bitches! If you don't want to die, you'd better do something! Aren't you bastards who feed on taxes? Where do you think that money comes from!"

The words, "I pay taxes too," rose to my tongue, but I couldn't say them.

However, perhaps because she still lacked experience, Assistant Clerk Lee Jiyeon couldn't hold back and talked back. "Hey, you think we don't pay taxes? And what's with 'fucking bitch'? Watch your mouth."

A sigh threatened to escape.

*She wasn't like this a year ago.*

The image of her from a year ago, beautiful and freshly appointed, was long gone. After spending her first month hounded by citizens and crying every day, she had completely succumbed to the black flames within three months.

While her world-weary face was locked in an argument with the citizen, I glanced at Senior Official Kim Changseok, who stood nearby.

*What about calling the police?* I asked with my eyes.

Senior Official Kim Changseok nodded. *I've reported it. They'll be here soon.*

How could we have such a conversation just by looking at each other? This wasn't the first or second time something like this had happened.

*I just need to stall for a little while.*

I suppressed a sigh and turned my head back toward the citizen.

"......"

A strange sound pierced my ears. The sensation that followed was immediate pain.

"Ghk..."

From the sudden pain, I instinctively lowered my head. A knife was lodged in my stomach.

*He… brought a knife?*

Why didn't anyone notice? Such a trivial thought was the first thing that came to mind. Well, who would have expected to get stabbed while working?

"KYAAAAAAAAH!!"

Senior Official Lee Jiyeon’s ear-splitting scream rang out.

"Heek."

The citizen who had just stabbed me pulled the knife out with a similarly shocked expression.

"Ghk..."

As the knife left the hole in my stomach, blood gushed from the wound. I hurriedly tried to cover the hole with both hands, but strength drained from my body. My legs gave out, and I collapsed to the floor as my consciousness slowly faded.

{sep}

In the early dawn, a young man who had been groaning in his sleep kicked off his blanket and sat up. Awake now, he muttered in a tone that didn't match his appearance.

"Hah. It's been a while since I had that dream, but it was damn realistic."

Seo Jihun. No, the young man who used the name Ilmok here had a strange experience a year ago. The moment he woke up, his personality had been replaced by that of a modern man from the Republic of Korea named Seo Jihun.

At first, he thought he was dreaming, but that thought faded as one day, then two, then a month passed.

*Reincarnation. No, is it spirit possession?*

It would be one thing if it had been since he was a baby. Since he was already fourteen when he opened his eyes, it felt closer to spirit possession than reincarnation.

Perhaps because the memory of his death as Seo Jihun was so intense, he often relived the experience as a nightmare even after the spirit possession. However, the frequency of those nightmares had decreased after he adapted to life here, but today, for some reason, he had that dream again for the first time in about half a year.

"Tsk. I should get to work."

He had woken up in the early dawn, but the nightmare had startled his brain, and he couldn't fall back asleep. A year had passed since he woke up here. It might seem strange for a fifteen-year-old boy to be working, but in ‘this world’, it wasn't anything special.

This world, where I had possessed Ilmok, was not the modern era.

*To think I'd wake up in a martial arts world of all places.*

As someone who valued work-life balance, I used to enjoy reading webnovels and webtoons. Well, it was more like I enjoyed them because my work-life balance was never maintained. Reading them on public transport during my commute was a great help in relieving stress.

Perhaps thanks to that experience, I adapted without much difficulty despite the spirit possession into a martial arts world.

Honestly, I even had a thought like that.

*Did I, without realizing it, leave a malicious comment on some martial arts novel?*

I wondered if I had been dragged into a novel I'd left a hate comment on. However, after looking into it, this world didn't seem connected to any novel I had read. There were no protagonists or major villains that I remembered.

In the end, I gave up on that idea and was living a life of moderate adaptation to this world. As a waiter.

Dammit, the Ilmok I had possessed was an orphan. Recalling Ilmok's memories, he wasn't an orphan from birth. He had become one a few months before my spirit possession. There weren't many things a fourteen-year-old orphan with no one to turn to could do, so I had no choice but to take the job of a waiter to eat and killing intent.

And once I actually started this job, it wasn't that difficult.

The two most important things a waiter needs in this world are perceptiveness and patience. First, you had to gauge a customer's wealth or abilities just by their appearance, clothing, and movements. If you carelessly let in the wrong customer and they decided to dine and dash, the waiter had to bear all the losses.

However, if you simply chased them away or ignored them based on their ragged clothes, you could face great trouble. Their clothes might be worn and covered in dust from a long journey, but they could be a master of martial arts or from a capable family.

And patience was necessary for a simple reason. It was because of the customers' power trips. Moreover, since this was a martial arts world, it was a half-lawless land. It was a place where you could get a knife in the gut for not enduring a customer's power trip and talking back.

*I can't let what happened in my past life happen again.*

In that sense, my skills honed from working as a civil servant for five years were more than enough to be a waiter. I was equipped with the patience I'd gained from dealing with citizen complaints and the perceptiveness I'd developed to survive in the damnably rigid civil servant society.

I put those skills to full use, starting work from the early dawn. First, upon waking, I go out to the yard and start the day by sweeping the inn entrance with a broom. After that, I return inside the inn to wipe the tables and clean the floor, and just then, I see the inn's owner, Mr. Taehyun, coming down.

"Hm? Ilmok. You're up earlier than usual today."

"My eyes just opened, so since I was up anyway, I thought I'd do some cleaning."

"Hahahaha. If I could just find one more waiter like you, Ilmok, I think our Cheongpung inn could become the best inn in Cheonyang Prefecture."

I gave a faint smile at Mr. Taehyun's words. The fact that I, who woke up in the body of a fourteen-year-old orphan, have survived until now was largely thanks to that man's help. He did more than just hire me as a waiter. He also provided me with a place to sleep and meals. He's even teaching me how to read, telling me to get a more respectable job when I grow up. Where could I find a better benefactor?

After greeting Mr. Taehyun, I focused on cleaning again. Soon it was time to open for the morning. I take orders from the customers, give the orders to my boss who is both the inn owner and the chef, and serve the food he prepares. After the customers leave, I clear the tables and then welcome new customers.

I repeated the same actions dozens of times, like a squirrel on a wheel.

"Hoo. Is the morning rush over?"

As the stream of customers trickled to a stop, I wiped my forehead and sighed. Now, all I had to do was clear the dishes from the last customers, and the morning's work would be done. Then there would be a short break before the evening service began.

I considered taking a short nap but didn't feel I needed one. Even though being a waiter is a physically demanding job, it wasn't tiring at all. One might think it's because I've gotten used to the work, but it had always been like this since I first started.

*Is it because I'm young? I just don't get tired.*

Even though I had worked from early morning after a poor night's sleep, my body was still full of energy.

Just as I was about to clear the last table, a sudden order came in.

"Bring me a bowl of the quickest noodles you can make."

I hadn't felt anyone enter the inn, but when I looked toward the voice, I saw an old man sitting at a table.

*He's a martial artist.*

I knew it instinctively. However, that made it all the more strange. Working as a waiter, I had seen martial artists several times, and every single one of them exuded an unapproachable aura, but from this old man, I couldn't feel any aura at all.

"Understood, Elder."

Nevertheless, I replied to the old man in a very respectful tone. This was a world where if you ignored your instincts, you never knew when you might get stabbed.

"Boss! One bowl of somyeon over here!"

After I relayed the order to the kitchen, about fifteen minutes passed. A plate of somyeon, along with a teapot and a teacup, came out from the kitchen. I carefully placed the somyeon and tea on the old man's table and stepped back. The old man calmly took a sip of tea, then picked up the noodles with his chopsticks and put them in his mouth.

After tasting a bite of the noodles and a sip of the tea, the old man spoke in a calm tone.

"Poison. It seems the Alliance has caught our tail."

"!?"

Before I could even ask what he meant, a gruesome sound came from the kitchen.

SHUK!

I had no time to turn my head in that direction. An energy like Mount Tai began to flow from the old man, from whom I had felt no presence until now.

While the old man glared at me, exuding a terrifying energy, a voice I'd never heard before came from the kitchen.

"The tail has been dealt with."

*The tail? Surely, he's not talking about the boss, is he?*

As that ominous thought crossed my mind, the old man looked me over as if appraising my worth.

He uttered something out of the blue. "Oho. You have quite a useful body to be living as a waiter in a place like this."

"??"

Before I could say something in response to the old man's highly misleading words, he suddenly vanished from the table.

FWIP!

The old man appeared right in front of my nose. He aimed a sword he had drawn at some point at my neck.

"Choose. Would you rather die here, or become my disciple?"

In this urgent situation, with a martial artist holding a sword to my neck, a trivial thought came to mind.

*Was the dream I had at dawn a premonition...*

It seemed I was well and truly fucked.
