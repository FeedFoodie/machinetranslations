I continued walking through Wensu County with Jin Hayeon and Branch Manager Ma Gangil. I observed the villagers and occasionally asked them questions.

After about two hours of this patrol-that-wasn't-a-patrol, the sky had turned completely black. Most residents had returned home. With the streets nearly empty, we went back to the Branch.

Jin Hayeon walked beside me and asked, "Young Lord. Have you come up with any ideas?"

Branch Manager Ma Gangil also looked at me, intrigued.

"If you're asking about ideas, I have a few."

*I said a few, but honestly, I have more than enough.*

It was only natural. From my perspective, having lived in the modern era, the way of life here was far too archaic. There were countless things that needed changing, but I had no intention of touching them all. Doing so would only bring about a terrible hell of work.

I had only one concern.

*Which of these things should I tackle to satisfy Master just enough? And on top of that, how can I avoid getting assigned additional work?*

I walked on, mulling over the issue, when a strange sensation made me turn my head.

"???"

Branch Manager Ma Gangil stood there dumbfounded, staring at me with a shocked expression. Jin Hayeon was not as surprised as Branch Manager Ma Gangil, but she also looked a little taken aback.

*He is surprised, right…?*

I had spent the last fifteen days with her, so I could tell her expression was slightly different.

"Is there a problem?" I asked hesitantly.

The astonished Branch Manager Ma Gangil replied, "You truly are the disciple chosen by the Supreme Lord. To think you found not just one, but several things to change in just two hours."

"Ah…"

I finally realized my mistake. Branch Manager Ma Gangil had probably worked here for years. From his perspective, my words could have sounded quite offensive.

But like the devout fanatic he was, he showed no offense or jealousy. He just seemed endlessly impressed. So, the mistake I was thinking of was not about hurting his feelings.

*...I need to be more careful.*

It was just like the dinner party, and now today. Things that were normal to me were astonishing to these people.

*I thought they'd only be surprised by scientific things like smartphones, TVs, or at the very least, light bulbs.*

There was a cliché I had often seen in an old Japanese manga or novel.

[Ah. This is what you call a lighter.]

[Ah. This is what you call a K-2 rifle.]

It was a common story. Modern items amazing people in a fantasy world. As a liberal arts major, I could not make a lighter, let alone a rifle, so I had thought it was irrelevant to me.

When I first fell into this world, I thought of democracy, but the imperial family held firm power here. Shouting about democracy was a surefire way to be branded a traitor and killed. I was just a waiter, so I kept my mouth shut and pretended to know nothing. I had not realized it then.

My liberal arts knowledge could also be a source of wonder to these people.

It was a problem I needed to be careful about. In this world, I was a parentless orphan and a former waiter. Knowing such things was bound to be suspicious.

"As expected. The Supreme Lord’s eyes are precise."

The two fanatics beside me were simply moved. They thought, ‘The disciple chosen by the great Heavenly Demon!!’

*I'm glad they're idiots.*

I sighed in relief and subtly changed the subject.

"By the way, Branch Manager Ma Gangil, you don't seem to be suffering from the side effects of demonic art. Have you, by any chance, succeeded in reaching the Transcendent Demon Realm?"

I had observed him for a while. He always laughed heartily with the villagers, a cheerful sound that did not match his rugged face. He did not seem like a demonic practitioner at all.

At my question, Branch Manager Ma Gangil frantically waved his arms, as if the idea was absurd.

"Hahaha. How could a mere Branch Manager like me have entered the Transcendent Demon Realm? It's just that the martial arts I learned has weak side effects, so it's not very noticeable, Young Lord. Hahaha."

A demonic art with weak side effects piqued my curiosity.

"If it's not too rude, may I ask what the side effect of your demonic art is?"

"Hahahaha. It's nothing much. There's just a minor side effect where I somehow keep feeling good. Hahaha."

"……"

*The side effect seemed to be mania.*

"Actually, records say that if I reach the Ultimate Demon Realm with this art, I'll keep laughing even in the most depressing situations and go insane. I could even have a seizure from laughing too hard, but my talent is lacking, so I haven't reached that realm yet. My goodness. I don't know if this is fortunate or unfortunate. Hahahahaha."

"……"

Perhaps because I now knew his condition, his hearty laughter suddenly felt pitiful.

{sep}

After we returned to the Branch, Jin Hayeon gave me new information about the Demonic Cult.

She explained that people like Branch Manager Ma Gangil, who constantly interact with local residents, generally learn ‘relatively’ safe demonic arts. Branch members typically learn arts with side effects unlikely to harm others, or their mastery is not very high.

The dangerous ones from the Demonic Academy learn high-level demonic arts and reach high realms. They usually join the martial forces to patrol Xinjiang, go on missions, or work at the Heavenly Demon Divine Cult's main headquarters.

It made sense. Placing people with side effects like Eldest Senior Brother's anger issues or the third Senior Brother's paranoia near civilians could cause a terrible disaster.

She added that it was rare, but not unheard of, for those outside the Demonic Academy to raise their realm and achievements enough to move from a Branch to a martial corps or the Main Headquarters.

"Have all your questions been answered?"

I nodded, and she rummaged through her bundle, pulling out books, ink, and paper.

"Why are you taking those out?"

"Now that you've finished your tasks for the day, isn't it time for your doctrinal studies?"

"Ah…"

I had been run ragged since morning with movement arts training and then patrolled the village all afternoon. I had completely forgotten she followed me here for my studies.

"Makes me want to write in a diary."

Jin Hayeon heard my frustrated mutter, tilted her head, and asked, "What did you say, Young Lord?"

I had mumbled in Korean, so Jin Hayeon could not understand.

{sep}

The next morning, after bathing and circulating my qi, it was time for a pleasant breakfast with Master.

"Hohoho. Please, eat comfortably."

The Master smiled benevolently, but threw a question at me as soon as I picked up my spoon.

"So, did anything come to mind after you looked around the village yesterday?"

*As expected. A Master who doesn't know how to separate mealtime from work.*

I swallowed a sigh, put down my spoon, and spoke. "I've thought of one thing, Master."

"Oho. I'm quite curious what you've thought of."

Branch Manager Ma Gangil also put down his spoon and looked at me curiously. I looked at the two expectant men and began to speak calmly.

"We should build a school in every village. Our Divine Cult's Branch can teach the young children."

"A school... Is there a particular reason you want to build a school?"

"The biggest reason is Branch Manager Ma Gangil's story."

The Master's gaze immediately shifted to Branch Manager Ma Gangil. Under that burdensome gaze, Branch Manager Ma Gangil let out a manic ‘Hahaha.’ I quickly added an explanation.

"Didn't the Branch Manager say it at dinner yesterday? Most residents here can't read, so he teaches the doctrine verbally."

"Hmm. He did say that, but if that's the case, shouldn't you teach the adults to read too, not just the children?"

"There's no need for that. They're already devout believers. Besides, they're too busy making a living to have time to study."

"You say the adults are already devout believers. That implies the children are not."

"!!!"

A startled Branch Manager Ma Gangil shot up from his chair. He prostrated on the floor and began slamming his head down.

"It's my failing, Cult Leader! I'll work more actively to proselytize to the younger children!"

Ma Gangil's forehead started to bleed. I quickly shouted, "It's not the Branch Manager's fault! The situation has changed!"

"The situation has changed… Can you explain what you mean?"

*Cold sweat trickled down my back. The Master won't harm me right now, but I don't know what will happen to Branch Manager Ma Gangil if I say the wrong thing.*

"Our Heavenly Demon Divine Cult's method of expansion is simple. We protected the people of Xinjiang from villains and taught them the doctrine, but nearly two hundred years have passed. Now, there are almost no bandits or mountain thieves left in Xinjiang. I heard they're practically extinct here in Wensu County, which is close to the Main Headquarters."

"So the changed situation you mentioned means the number of villains has decreased?"

"That's correct. The older residents feel infinite gratitude toward our Cult, but the children can't empathize with that. Furthermore, human nature is fundamentally evil. If a favor continues, people will take it as a right."

I added the famous line from a movie to my explanation, and Master nodded in agreement. My words aligned with a core principle of the Demonic Cult's doctrine, ‘Human nature is fundamentally evil.’

"So, you're saying the children, who don't remember receiving our help, might take our Cult's goodwill for granted."

"That's correct, Master. Our Cult also collects donations, though less than the wicked imperial family. As time passes, someone will inevitably wonder, ‘Why do we have to pay donations to the Heavenly Demon Divine Cult?’"

"That is certainly a plausible event."

The Master replied as if he understood, then looked at the kneeling Branch Manager Ma Gangil. "Branch Manager, you may rise. As my disciple said, you are not at fault."

"Ah, I understand, Heavenly Demon."

Ma Gangil stood up, still dazed, and looked at me with admiration. His shocked stare, combined with the blood on his forehead, made his gaze even more burdensome than usual.

The Master seemed unconcerned. He glanced at Branch Manager Ma Gangil, then looked back at me. "In the end, you're saying the martial artists of each Branch should teach the village children letters and doctrine."

"That's correct. People are greatly influenced by what they experience as children. They will be grateful for learning to read, and if they study the doctrine from a young age, they will very likely become faithful believers."

"But you've overlooked one thing. As you said, the common people are barely making a living. Will the children, who are busy helping their families, have time to attend school?"

"They're the solution," I replied, pointing at Branch Manager Ma Gangil. "Aren't the branch members just helping with odd jobs now that the villains are gone? The members who aren't teaching can simply do the chores the children would have done."

"Hohoho. Indeed, you've thought of everything. Your outstanding insight, combined with the side effect of the Soul-Reaping Heartless Sword, produces a truly excellent result."

"It... it seems so. Ha, haha." I could not correct Master's misunderstanding, so I just laughed awkwardly.

The broad framework was set. We would build a school in every Xinjiang village with a Heavenly Demon Divine Cult Branch and teach the children the doctrine. The Master and I then began to discuss the details.

"First, we should teach the Thousand Character Classic. Even young children should be able to master it in about a year."

"That's a sound idea, but as you know, the scripture, the Records of the Heavenly Demon, often contains characters not found in the Thousand Character Classic."

"In any case, much of the Records of the Heavenly Demon is too difficult for children. We need to create a simplified doctrine book for them, using only characters from the Thousand Character Classic."

"Hmm. They'll be learning the Thousand Character Classic for a year. We can create the simplified doctrine book during that time."

"Yes. If we teach the children this way for about two years, all the children of Xinjiang will be reborn as followers of the Heavenly Demon Divine Cult."

"Hohoho. In that case, the task of creating that simplified doctrine book..."

I quickly cut in before Master could finish. "That requires research from those who have served the cult for a long time and have mastered the scriptures. I'd like to take charge since I proposed the idea, but I haven't even learned half the scriptures yet. If I stepped forward, I might teach incorrect content."

*I can't take on another task.*

*I chose the 'school' idea precisely for this reason. The regional branch members would run the schools. It has nothing to do with me.*

*The simplified doctrine book is the same. I'm just beginning to learn the doctrine, so I can't possibly create it.*

"Indeed, this task seems a bit premature for you to handle, Ilmok."

I almost shouted with joy at Master's words.

*I dodged the work this time!*

I had achieved my modest goal. If this conversation dragged on, I risked getting caught in another trap. I quickly spoke up.

"Master. The food is getting cold."

"Ahem. Now that you mention it, it is. I'll look into the additional matters when I return to the cult. Let's enjoy our meal for now."

It felt more like a meeting, but we were strictly in the middle of ‘breakfast’.

But even with Master's permission, a comfortable meal was difficult.

"Um, Branch Manager Ma Gangil, maybe you should get your head treated before you eat?"

Branch Manager Ma Gangil was shoveling food into his mouth, ignoring the trail of red blood on his forehead.

But at my considerate words, Branch Manager Ma Gangil just laughed heartily. "Hahaha. It's fine. It's already dried, so it shouldn't be a big deal. More importantly, you truly are the Supreme Lord and his disciple. I was so engrossed listening to things I could never have imagined that I didn't even realize my forehead was bleeding. Hahaha."

Branch Manager Ma Gangil was just that cheerful.
