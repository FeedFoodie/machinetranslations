"First, we must create a clear reporting system and standardized report forms," I answered confidently, as if I knew everything in the world. I was just a fifteen-year-old brat who had been in the Heavenly Demon Divine Cult for less than ten days.

"Once we establish a reporting system and forms, handling matters will become easier. For example, the Senior Brothers won't need to personally run around the prefectures to understand the situation like this time. They can just review reports submitted to the higher-ups and check for discrepancies."

I continued my explanation, basing it on the modern practices of civil servants in the Republic of Korea. "We need to specialize. We can't have just anyone handle every task in a region. This is where a specific reporting system and fixed forms will prove their strength."

Dividing tasks through compartmentalization and specialization would naturally create dozens of departments. "Each department in the field can then report the situation using these forms and handle their duties based on reports from the higher-ups."

This would lead to buck-passing, just like in the administrative departments of the Republic of Korea. Work would be delayed as reports traveled from the field to the top. Reports would often vanish entirely when mid-level managers intervened to avoid ‘responsibility’.

"Furthermore, if someone handles the same duties for too long, they might become corrupt. We should make them change their assigned region or department regularly. This will prevent them from forming close relationships with local influential figures or people in related industries."

This was the part of the job that Korean civil servants found most burdensome. Department transfers were conducted under the pretext of preventing corruption, but in reality, they were what civil servants struggled with most. The biggest reason was the lack of a proper handover. Only the department changed.

"In any case, with a detailed reporting system and standardized forms, there shouldn't be any problem carrying out duties even after transferring departments or regions."

After a department transfer, the answer from a predecessor was always the same.

"Just read the manual."

"You'll find everything if you look through the forms."

"I'm busy adjusting to my new position, so why do you keep calling me? Figure it out yourself."

Right. That was understandable. Just as I had been unexpectedly dragged to another department, they had also been forcibly moved. Even so.

*Then where are all the reports and files you made while you worked here? I was hoping to study them...*

"Why would I leave those? I made them. Of course I moved them all to a USB and deleted them."

*…...*

He could have at least left some trace of the work he did.

*You bastards!!*

Memories of my time as a civil servant surfaced, and I almost shouted by mistake. Thanks to that damn predecessor, I suffered through days and nights of overtime, digging through manuals and forms just to adapt. My life was a billion light-years away from the work-life balance I had dreamed of.

The civil service's work method was full of problems, despite its glossy justifications. This method obsessed over responsibility and relied only on fixed manuals and forms. It was fast for routine tasks but dragged on endlessly with exceptional situations or civil complaints.

And could you uncover corruption just by comparing reports to the situation on the ground? Unless they were idiots, anyone with a few years of experience could manipulate things enough to line their own pockets. It was said to be less common these days, but any civil servant at the Team Leader level or above in real estate, architecture, or construction could skim off the top if they wanted. The system introduced to prevent that corruption was the department transfer. It was the worst system, the one that drove low-level civil servants insane.

The reason I so confidently explained this flawed system was simple. I wanted them to notice its flaws. Even if they didn't, it didn't matter as long as the system was unsuitable for the Demonic Cult.

Before I died, the phrase ‘Who’s holding a knife to your throat?’ became popular on an online community. I remembered a post there from a company’s HR manager. It was a mix of grievances and advice for new employees and interns, and the gist was simple.

The point was that new hires or interns shouldn't be overzealous. A rookie who hadn't adapted to the company yet would only look pathetic trying to bring innovation. If you wanted to lead change, you first had to completely grasp the company’s operating system.

What the company's assistant managers and above wanted from a new employee was just one thing. They wanted the employee to quickly adapt to the work and understand how the company operated.

So I acted in reverse, like the new employee in that post. I was a rookie who had been here less than ten days and didn't understand how the Demonic Cult operated. I acted important and arrogantly laid out a new, flawed system as if it were some great innovation.

*Now they'll see me as pathetic, right?*

I wanted them to see me as a guy who works hard and is somewhat smart, but lacks foresight. Just another overzealous newcomer.

I wanted everyone to see me that way, but.

*…What is this?*

For some reason, Master looked at me with utter delight.

"Hahahahaha! Master. Now I see the Youngest is much smarter than I thought." Eldest Senior Brother laughed foolishly, overjoyed.

"……"

"……"

Third Senior Brother and Sixth Senior Brother said nothing. One glared at me darkly, while the other looked at me with surprise.

"To even consider corruption among those in the field. Master, isn't this a system that keeps our Divine Cult’s doctrines in mind?"

As if that wasn't enough, the foolishly laughing Eldest Senior Brother Wi Jinhak spouted this strange nonsense.

"Hohoho. You are right. Humans are inherently evil. However, I, a heavenly demonic practitioner, can't personally check on every cultist every day. To think it can be solved so simply by transferring departments and regions. Hohoho."

The Master also chuckled, satisfied with the idea.

It was unfair. I hadn't connected this problem with the doctrine.

*I didn't want to be brainwashed, so I never even glanced at the doctrinal texts except when studying.*

Had I already been brainwashed without realizing it?

*No. That's not it. No matter what nonsense I spout, these people will connect it to the doctrine.*

Anyone deeply engrossed in religion is like that, heretic or not. When something happens, they connect it to a verse from the Bible or a Buddhist sutra and think, ‘This too must be the will of the heavens.’

But that wasn't important right now. Why were these people listening so intently to a clueless rookie’s ramblings? The dark glare from Third Senior Brother was especially chilling.

*I guess I can forget about sleeping peacefully from now on.*

I felt he would sneak into my quarters on a moonlit night and unleash the Fourteen Shadowless Combat Art on my neck. I swallowed dryly at the chilling thought.

"Hahahaha. Well, well. It seems the Youngest is smarter than us Senior Brothers. To avoid being a shameful Senior Brother, I must strive forward, and strive again."

A torrent of tactless praise poured from Eldest Senior Brother.

I sighed inwardly. My attempt to look foolish had only drawn more attention. Then, Master asked me a question.

"Just as Jinhak said, it is a truly excellent idea. However, I find it a bit too difficult for you, Ilmok, an orphan and former inn waiter, to have come up with on your own."

As soon as he finished, the Senior Brothers’ gazes focused on me again. Their eyes seemed to ask about my true identity. I had tried to act like a fool showing off shallow knowledge, but instead, they were cornering me as a suspicious genius.

Perhaps it was the life-threatening fear, but my mind spun faster than usual, and a decent excuse came to me.

"While listening to you, Master, and the Senior Brothers, I wondered how we could handle things more easily, but for some reason, my thoughts didn't stop at a reasonable point. I kept thinking until I found a ‘perfect’ method."

Thankfully, Master responded just as I intended. "Oho. Your thoughts didn't stop until a perfect method came to mind, you say. This is likely a side effect of the Soul-Reaping Heartless Sword."

"Ah! Now that you mention it, I think it really is because of that martial art."

I had successfully acted like a rookie swayed by a demonic art’s side effect, one who didn't even realize the art was the cause.

As if my acting had worked perfectly, Master muttered, "Indeed," and stroked his beard. The Senior Brothers’ gazes naturally turned back to Master.

The Master seemed to organize his thoughts, then burst into a hearty laugh. "Hohoho. It seems I have found a proper disciple in my later years. It hasn't even been ten days since your initiation, yet a side effect has already appeared. Your mental and martial arts achievements are both excellent."

At Master’s high praise, the Senior Brothers’ gazes focused on me again.

"…"

The ‘youngest disciple hides his talent’ strategy was now out the window. This was no different from the ‘youngest disciple flaunts his talent’.

*Or it could become ‘youngest disciple flaunts his talent and gets his head lopped off by a Senior Brother’...*

While the gazes of Master and the Senior Brothers troubled me, Master added one last thing.

"Hohoho. Thinking about it, we’ve talked too much about work at a gathering meant to foster camaraderie. We should discuss the details separately. For today, let’s just enjoy the drinks. I will call for you later, Ilmok, to organize the matters we discussed."

My overtime following overtime was confirmed.

{sep}

Unlike a typical old boomer, Master didn't bring up work again. The only problem was the clueless Eldest Senior Brother Wi Jinhak, who kept praising me. Each time he did, I had to meet Third Senior Brother’s chilling gaze.

Fortunately or not, no special guests visited the Wind Cliff Pavilion for the next three days. Third Senior Brother didn't visit with a knife between his teeth on a moonlit night, and Master hadn't summoned me for overtime yet.

During those three days, I talked with Jin Hayeon and came to understand why everyone had treated me like a genius.

*I had no idea they were handling things so crudely...*

After subtly questioning Jin Hayeon, I learned that the Heavenly Demon Divine Cult handled its work by rule of thumb.

When I thought about it, this was only natural. The civil service system of the Republic of Korea, however criticized, was far more sophisticated than the system of a medieval martial arts fantasy world.

Moreover, this was a heretic group, not the Imperial Palace. It was the kind of place where if the Cult Leader said, ‘Do it!’ they would just answer, ‘Yes!’ and proceed without any system.

That was why they looked so shocked when they heard the flawed administrative system I spouted, like primitive people encountering a new civilization.

*Damn it.*

I had my excuses. It was a hastily prepared dinner, so I hadn't grasped all these underlying circumstances.

Most importantly, I had unknowingly regarded Master as too great a figure. It wasn't that I was engrossed in the heretic doctrine. The problem was the dignity Master occasionally showed. I had rashly assumed that such a great leader would naturally have some sort of system in place. I had overlooked that a person from the old days was still a person from the old days, no matter how great.

However, what was past was past. I couldn't neglect my training. I had to survive the grasping hands of the Senior Brothers and, more importantly, survive the Demonic Academy examination next year.

Furthermore, Master had said he would make me work overtime, so I risked losing more time soon.

And so, on the fourth day, as I reluctantly went about my days worrying about a potential guest, one finally arrived.

It was a cute young lady who looked to be in her early teens at most.

"What brings you all the way here, Young Lady Seon'ah?"
