First, the song was good. If K-Pop is divided into typical idol songs and easy-listening tracks aiming for extreme public appeal, then 『When I was ()』, commonly called 'When I was', belonged to neither.

If you had to put a label on it, it was a dance track with strong indie music colors and a slow tempo?

Things with a solid identity usually have short names. The more ambiguous the identity, the longer the description becomes. A dance track with strong indie music colors and a slow tempo is a case in point.

It was a genre that had not yet been given a clear 'This is it!' label. It seemed to borrow the rhythm of R&B while using a unique texture... but it was 'good to listen to' music.

When the conclusion is good, uniqueness is reframed as individuality. The intro, which passed the test in the first five seconds, played the role of keeping the listener hooked enough to continue watching the music video.

The second reason for its success appears at this point.

His stunning looks, honed to perfection, a physique that had never seen a double-digit body fat percentage thanks to years of management, and the music video, which I and the dedicated team carved out with our very souls as chisels, combined with the artistic choreography to leave a powerful visual impression.

Kang Kiyeon's music video, inspired by a captivating photo of a deep blue sea, was born like this.

At one time, Kang Kiyeon ran forward, dreaming only of a future where he would become an idol and shine on stage. The dream he had became a paper sea sparkling under a blue sky in the music video.

Reality was not so easy. Kang Kiyeon's wish was not easily granted, so he had to take a long detour.

The vinyl-packaged seawater that arrived in a cardboard box, even when poured into a bathtub, only reflected the gray ceiling, looking murky. The sea projected onto the concrete by a beam projector was blurry and frozen, fixed like a photograph.

Nothing could give Kang Kiyeon a breath of fresh air. In his small room, Kang Kiyeon slowly became drenched, unable to feel the presence of the sea at all.

Just as he had been when he was trapped in the abyss of the vast ocean, sinking deep with a great sense of anxiety.

Water droplets falling from his black hair left marks on the floor that would not dry. They gathered and grew, slowly swallowing the floor Kang Kiyeon stood on.

When Kang Kiyeon lifted his head, a square hole in the ceiling came into view. It was filled with something like the sea in the photograph, something that did not flow downward.

The star-studded eyes of an idol were gone. In Kang Kiyeon's eyes, only the shadow of what seemed to be a rippling wave was reflected.

The camera's point of view shifted from top to bottom. A transparent blue light was cast over Kang Kiyeon's face, as if filmed through a blue cellophane sheet.

Reaching his arm toward the hole, Kang Kiyeon tore off the wire mesh. Then he extended his fingers and dug into the dry-textured object.

Crumbs, too lifeless to be a sea but too beautiful to be a fantasy, fell slowly.

Past the cardboard box with its shipping label smudged, the overflowing bathtub, and the stained gray walls.

A small, square object was captured against a pure white background. The giant acrylic box filled with blue gel was reminiscent of an ant farm kit.

Only, there were no ants.

No paths were made either. Only a small groove carved into one wall hinted at Kang Kiyeon's world below.

Since we could not just show similar scenes for the entire four minutes, I had to think about how to create a twist while keeping the keyword 'submersion' suggested by the composer.

I cannot count how many times we changed the props, because if each image did not connect organically, it would be nothing more than a simple sequence of scenes.

I was uncertain even as I wrote the proposal, but my worries were unfounded. The hands of experts turned my humble idea into a masterpiece with stunning visuals.

The final version, with solo dance scenes and group dances with backup dancers appropriately cross-edited, boasted flawless perfection.

As his solo activities were wrapping up, Kang Kiyeon started heading to the practice room after finishing his broadcast schedules. It was to prepare for spArk's final comeback of the year.

He had to learn the choreography in advance to join the group practice that would be starting soon.

During the short car ride from the broadcast station to the company, Kang Kiyeon read comments and watched fan videos. This was something unimaginable back when Kim Iwol had forbidden him from monitoring reactions.

Now, reading the posts was quite alright. There were negative reviews, but his mood was no longer swayed by such things.

The joy of reading the reviews from people who enjoyed it was much greater.

He got out of the car in a good mood and arrived at the practice room to find Choi Jeho waiting for him.

"You haven't gone home yet?"

"You said you were practicing alone."

It seemed he intended to watch over his moves before leaving. Kang Kiyeon gladly accepted his help. Thanks to learning the choreography during his breaks, the catch-up session ended quickly.

Wiping away his sweat, Kang Kiyeon asked, "Jeho-hyung. You're an idol because you love it, right?"

Choi Jeho replied, "Would I be doing it if I hated it?" It was a very Choi Jeho-like answer.

"Then you really are a genius, hyung. You do it because you love it, you're good at it, and you have no hesitation in pushing forward."

It was an extension of the conversation they had before his comeback. His tone suggested that while he seemed to have accepted Choi Jeho's words, he had not completely given up his own thoughts.

After much thought, Kang Kiyeon shared the conclusion he had reached.

"I'm still not sure... but this work is the most fun for me right now, so I think I'll try to keep doing what I like."

Kang Kiyeon recalled the last comment he saw. It was a post saying that they were proud of the time they spent cheering for him.

It seemed that no matter what the public's evaluation was, or even if he sometimes felt his own image was shabby, he would be okay as long as he remembered those words.

"Yes."

Leaving that one word, Choi Jeho stood up first. Was it because he thought Kang Kiyeon had properly understood what he was trying to say? There were no unnecessary additions.

Kang Kiyeon was left alone in the practice room. He lay down on his back on the spacious practice room floor.

The tension in his entire body melted away.

The cool air settled on the floor cooled his hot body. It felt like he could get addicted to the refreshing feeling.

The day he would regret his decision to come here would probably never come.
