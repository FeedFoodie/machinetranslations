"You want to do *what*?"

"I want to build a physical training room."

At Estella's words, the Marquis lowered the documents he was holding. She had come to his office, and he had expected another request for dresses or accessories, but a physical training room was the last thing he anticipated.

"Why on earth would you need that?" the Marquis asked, his curiosity genuinely piqued.

The request was simply too random, especially coming from Estella. He had, of course, received reports that she had been exercising diligently every morning. He also knew she had lost a considerable amount of weight. Her pursuit of a healthier lifestyle was a commendable thing, but he wondered if a dedicated training room was not taking it too far. Besides, the knights already had a perfectly good place for physical training set up on their grounds.

As if reading his mind, Estella spoke. "The training grounds the knights use aren't suitable for me."

The Marquis's eyes narrowed slightly. "Aren't you already running on those grounds every morning?"

"Running isn't enough anymore," she replied. "I have my own plan. Would you like to see it?"

Deciding a demonstration was worth more than a thousand words, Estella held out a scroll she had prepared. Her own drawing skills were nonexistent, so she had enlisted Ria's help to sketch her designs, adding copious notes to explain each one.

A bewildered Marquis took the scroll she offered. He gave his daughter one last distrustful look before reluctantly shifting his gaze to the parchment.

"Hm?"

On it were drawings of all sorts of strange objects he had never seen in his life. Adjusting his glasses, the Marquis found he could not take his eyes off the scroll for a long time.

{sep}

"Are you truly going to allow it?" Raul asked the Marquis with a puzzled expression after Estella had left the office.

The scroll she had brought was filled with unidentifiable objects. He wondered why such crude things were necessary when they already had swords and shields, perfectly good items for physical training.

"It's the first time," the Marquis said quietly.

"Pardon?"

"The first time Estella has ever come to me and said she wants to *do* something."

"Ah..." Understanding finally dawned on Raul. He now saw why the Marquis had so readily agreed after just one look at the scroll. The Marquis was genuinely happy about his eldest daughter's change. His gruff personality rarely showed it, but Raul, having served him for so long, could see just how proud he was of Estella in that moment.

Still, he couldn't help but worry, especially since the territory's financial situation was not at its best.

"She said she would sell her own jewels to fund it, so we will wait and see," the Marquis declared. "She must take responsibility for her words. There will be no funds from me."

Commendable as her initiative was, the Marquis wanted Estella to see this project through from start to finish on her own. He was also, on the other hand, quite curious to see just how much she could accomplish.

"I will provide the unused warehouse next to the training grounds," Raul offered. "I heard the roof leaks, so that will need repairs. I wonder if she'll need something like a tea table?"

Though Raul voiced his concern, he too was pleased by Estella's change and immediately began brainstorming things the young lady might need. A physical training room would surely require a space to rest during workouts.

"Do so," the Marquis replied with a short nod, turning his eyes back to his documents.

Raul, however, did not miss the slight upward curl of his master's lips. He also began busily jotting down furniture and items for the rest area in his notebook, his quill pen moving as if it had wings.

{sep}

Permission secured, Estella wasted no time. She gathered every jewel from her dressing room and sold them at the largest jewelry store in Winterfall Town. Perhaps because she went with Raul's introduction, the jeweler gave her a very generous price. With her now-bulging coin purse, she headed for the only blacksmith's forge in the territory.

BANG! BANG! BANG!

The rhythmic sound of a hammer striking iron grew louder as she approached.

Gai, the knight assigned by the Marquis to escort her, stood beside Estella with a displeased look on his face. 

*What a waste of time,* he thought. *I should be training.* 

He was already annoyed that she was building something right next to the training grounds.

*She should just stick to running. What is she even trying to do?*

The complaints rose up endlessly in his mind, but he kept his mouth shut. No matter how incompetent and ugly the young lady was, she was still the Marquis's daughter, and the Marquis was his lord.

*It would have been nice if I were assigned to escort Lady Catherine.*

Estella had lost quite a bit of weight, but Gai certainly didn't think she was slender or beautiful like her sister. Filled with these and other grievances, he followed silently behind her.

A wave of heat washed over them as they entered the forge, the air inside much hotter than outside due to the red-hot molten iron. The bodies of the blacksmiths, who hammered without rest, were drenched in sweat and glistened in the firelight. The air was thick with a complex mix of sweat, oil, and the scorched tang of heated metal. It was a foul stench that would make anyone frown, yet not a hint of displeasure showed on Estella's face. Instead, she looked around the forge with a curious expression, diligently taking everything in.

Dolan, the forge master, belatedly noticed their entrance. He put down the hammer he was holding and shot to his feet.

"Are you Dolan?" Estella asked, looking at the man who approached and bowed deeply.

He was said to be of dwarven descent and stood a handspan shorter than her. A forehead that looked as if a capital M had been carved into it and sharp, gull-wing eyebrows that seemed permanently raised in anger gave him a fierce impression.

"Yes, but what brings a noble to a place like this..."

A noble, especially a young lady, never set foot in a forge. Judging by her attire and the knight at her side, she was of high rank. Dolan's guard went up, wondering if she was here to stir up trouble.

"It's a pleasure to meet you. I'm Estella," she said. "I came because I have something I'd like to commission you to make. I'm sorry for showing up so suddenly without an appointment."

Far from causing trouble, Estella grabbed his dirty hand, shook it, and smiled brightly, leaving Dolan with a blank expression. She was even apologizing for coming unannounced. He had never seen or heard of a noble apologizing to a commoner.

*But Estella? I've heard that name somewhere... Huh?*

Belatedly realizing who she was, Dolan's eyes narrowed sharply. The rumors about Lady Estella among the territory's residents were not good at all. She was the foolish young lady who cared only for food. The dim-witted but greedy girl who always coveted her kind and pretty younger sister's things. The spendthrift who indulged in luxury without a care for the territory's situation and treated her servants poorly. The one who regarded the residents as insects and hated even looking at them. There wasn't a single good thing said about her.

And yet, this same young lady was holding his grime-covered hand without hesitation and smiling so brightly. 

*Were all those rumors just nonsense?*

"You're asking me to make... this?" Dolan asked, his skepticism clear.

It was strange enough that she had come to the forge herself, but the things Estella wanted him to make were even stranger. They were too blunt to be weapons and not nearly beautiful enough to be ornaments. They were certainly not items a noble lady would ever use.

"Yes. Make it just like this," Estella said, her expression alight with enthusiasm. "But this middle part here is the handle, so it would be good if you could cover it with leather. You can talk to the tanner about it."

She diligently explained the various pieces of equipment she wanted, pointing to each drawing and never forgetting to ask if Dolan understood correctly. The explanation continued for nearly an hour, with Dolan occasionally interjecting with questions of his own.

"The dum-bal can be made by simply melting a lump of iron, but..."

"It's dumbbell."

"Ahem. The dumbbell can be made by melting a lump of iron," Dolan corrected himself, "but there's a bit of a problem here." 

He tapped the drawing on the paper with his thick finger. He was confident he knew more about iron than anyone, but everything Lady Estella had brought were things he had never seen before. 

"To hang weight on such a long iron bar, the balance must be just right. If it's too heavy, it will be difficult to handle, and if it's too light, it won't be effective."

It was a reasonable point, and Estella nodded in agreement.

"That's why I want to make it so the weight can be adjusted."

"The weight, you say?" Dolan's voice rose unconsciously. The idea of freely adjusting the weight of a solid iron lump seemed nonsensical. He was being helplessly drawn into the conversation with Estella but didn't even realize it.

"That's right," she confirmed. "We'll make separate discs so they can be added or removed as needed. If you look at the drawing, you can see several discs of different sizes, right? Those are for adjusting the weight on the iron bar. They have holes in the middle, see? You just need to create a locking mechanism so they don't fall off when they're attached."

"But to make it like this, the cost..."

THUD!

Before Dolan could finish his sentence, Estella placed a heavy purse full of gold coins on the wooden table.
