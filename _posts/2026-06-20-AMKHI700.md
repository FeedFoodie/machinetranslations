spArk's youngest member. The team's main dancer. A handsome man who looks sharp, whose hobby is reading romance comics while dressed in the fashion of a handsome non-celebrity.

Kang Kiyeon had many attractive qualities. He had handsome features the public would acknowledge, yet touches of subculture were sprinkled in, making him seem multifaceted.

This was the exact opposite of a model student who might get a free pass at a formal meeting with the in-laws but could seem boring to some. His style, which made you want to emulate him despite his sharp looks, inspired admiration, while his sense for making even a cute sticker look cool and not childish sparked interest.

The psychology of imitation has a strange side to it. It does not automatically connect a good-looking subject with a subject one wants to imitate.

It is easy to understand using Choi Jeho as an example. Choi Jeho was the team's best clothes horse, standing out in whatever he wore, but not everyone wanted to have the same body type as that guy.

It meant that while the dance Choi Jeho performed with his body was cool, not many people dreamed of becoming the Choi Jeho of their neighborhood. Because to look as good as Choi Jeho in just a jersey, you needed ridiculously pumped-up shoulders.

Lee Cheonghyeon was similar. Just because Lee Cheonghyeon came out with a beautifully permed hairstyle, how many men would take a picture of Lee Cheonghyeon to their salon?

Just because Lee Cheonghyeon wore a sweatshirt with an adorable print on his handsome face, did that mean everyone would buy the same sweatshirt expecting a similar visual outcome?

On the other hand, Park Joowoo and Kang Kiyeon frequently appeared on accounts recommending men's styles. Jeong Seongbin was often mentioned as the epitome of the neat college student look.

It was not that the physiques of these three were common at all. I had ridden the subway to and from Gangnam until I was sick of it, but I could count on one hand the number of times I had seen men with such body types.

So why was the reaction so different? I would point to 'the presence or absence of a point that seems imitable' as the reason.

Park Joowoo was fond of a headset that anyone who listened to a bit of music would want to own at least one of. It was not so expensive as to be called a luxury item in the world of headsets, but the price was realistic enough to be a goal if you decided to save up for one.

Added to this were a sleeveless shirt and a zip-up hoodie, and the occasional fake piercings he wore on stage, creating a mysterious atmosphere.

Kang Kiyeon had his own unique hipness. For beginners whose playlists were full of rap or who wanted to style with leather but were not sure how to match it, Kang Kiyeon's daily looks were the perfect textbook.

When you saw the guitar case slung casually over his shoulder, did you not naturally imagine something like 'me, walking through a crowd with a guitar'?

Those who aimed for a clean-cut look often referenced Jeong Seongbin's airport fashion, variety show outfits, and photoshoots. By adding just a few key points here and there, they could significantly elevate the completeness of their outfits.

For example, Park Joowoo's headset was an old model he received as a gift, which gained fame as a vintage item that only those in the know would recognize. Kang Kiyeon would top off a casual style with a pair of basketball shoes, or masterfully save a potentially common look by matching a simple silhouette with a hat or a cardigan.

Jeong Seongbin owned several watches famous for their simple design and excellent technology, and enthusiasts detected this with an almost supernatural ability.

So why the long speech about the practicality of Kang Kiyeon and Omoknun2's fashion?
