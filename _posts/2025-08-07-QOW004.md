Estella walked for about an hour, her mind drifting back to the morning runs she once took in the military.

*A brisk walk like this is nothing.*

She focused on controlling her breathing, falling into the old rhythm.

*One, one, two, two.*

She drew two sharp breaths in through her nose, then released them in two controlled exhales through her mouth. Any disruption to the pattern would shatter her pace and drain her strength. Though she could no longer run as she used to, Estella did her best to maintain the technique, her steps falling into a steady, rhythmic beat.

If she had her way, she would have run for two or even three hours, but her stamina was the problem. Her weak bones strained to support the unfamiliar weight of her body. After an hour of walking, a tingling sensation shot through her legs while her knees and ankles screamed in protest.

*Completing a marathon used to be easy, but now even a short walk like this is a struggle.*

She had expected this, but her body was in even worse shape than she had imagined. Still, Estella decided not to overdo it.

*Exercise is a long-term game. I can't just move recklessly.*

Drenched in sweat as if she had been caught in a downpour, Estella finally stopped. Her ankles and knees throbbed with a dull ache, and she gasped for air as if she had just finished a full sprint.

It was a truly pathetic body.

"My lady, are you all right?"

Ria's worried voice cut through her ragged breaths. She had approached at some point and now held out a towel soaked in cold water.

"I'm fine," Estella panted, her expression brighter than ever as she took the towel and wiped her face. "This is just the beginning."

"Here, have some water."

While Estella was still catching her breath, Ria poured the lemon water she had prepared into a cup and offered it to her. Slices of lemon floated inside the cool liquid.

"Thank you."

Estella gulped down the water, then took the handkerchief Ria handed her to wipe the remaining sweat from her face.

"Ria," she said, her breathing finally evening out, "could you contact Lily's dress shop? I need to have a new dress made."

"A dress?"

"I've thought about it, and I just can't bring myself to wear that horrible thing."

Ria looked puzzled. The lady had always worn whatever Lady Catherine had made for her without complaint, but when she met Estella's clear, determined gaze, she simply nodded.

"Yes! I understand."

"Estella is exercising?"

Marquis Cassian looked up from the document he was reading, his expression surprised. He pushed his gold-rimmed glasses up the bridge of his nose, the lenses glinting in the sunlight as he questioned his butler, Raul. His eyesight had worsened recently, forcing him to have them fitted, and they proved more serviceable than he had feared. For a man who had held a sword his entire life, the need for glasses was a strange reality, but these days he spent more time with documents than with a blade.

He wondered if he had misheard Raul's report.

Then again, Estella's body was not one that could be mistaken for anyone else's.

He did think something had seemed different during their meal together, the first they had shared in a long time. She had eaten much less than usual then, too.

"Yes, Your Excellency," Raul confirmed. The usually taciturn man seemed somewhat excited as he spoke, the brown eyes behind his own round, silver-rimmed glasses sparkling. "Lady Estella walked around the training grounds for about an hour this morning. She also had a light breakfast, unlike her usual."

"We'll see how long it lasts."

The marquis's interest faded as quickly as it had appeared, and he turned his intense stare back to his documents. It was not long, however, before his hand paused its movement, hovering for a moment before he lowered his gaze again as if nothing had happened.

Raul did not miss his master's subtle change. It was a habit that surfaced whenever he was deep in thought. The marquis was a warm-hearted man, but like most who had spent their lives on the battlefield, he was clumsy at expressing his emotions. With years that had frosted both their hair white and forced glasses upon their eyes, a deep, unspoken understanding had grown between them.

*At times like this, it should be fine for me, the butler, to give a little push.*

"How about offering her some praise this time?" Raul suggested gently.

"I'll watch a little longer first."

Even with the gentle prodding, the marquis remained blunt. Raul, however, was not one to give up so easily.

"Still, she seems a bit different this time."

Instead of answering, the marquis tapped the quill he was holding against the paper, leaving a small trail of inky dots.

The season when the demonic beasts would rampage was fast approaching.

The marquis swallowed a sigh as he looked at the date. Every year they raised the castle walls and strengthened their defenses, but the villages far from that protection were often left defenseless. Worse, the attacks grew more frequent with each passing year, their patterns lacking any regularity.

The demonic beasts themselves had become more cruel. In the past, their main targets had been livestock, but now they descended upon entire villages, leaving nothing alive, be it crops or animals.

Their weapons and armor were poor, and raising the walls was not a fundamental solution. The number of soldiers was dwindling from frequent sorties, and recruitment was not easy, forcing them to hire mercenaries. The faces of the soldiers who had returned just yesterday were etched with the fatigue and fear of men who had stared death in the face.

A heavy worry settled on the marquis's features as he gazed out the window.

The area immediately surrounding his castle had not suffered direct damage, but that did not mean he could ignore the suffering of his people. The damage was especially severe near the forest where the demonic beasts swarmed.

"Perhaps it would be better to accept the imperial family's proposal," he murmured.

But despite the words he uttered like a sigh, his affection for Cardein was not something he could give up so easily. It was a territory that had been passed down through his family for nearly a hundred years.

Winterfall had not always been a land where demonic beasts ran rampant. Only in the last five years had their movements become so unusually active, bringing poor harvests in their wake. There had been no drought, nor had they suffered massive damage from floods, yet when harvest time came, the fields were invariably ruined by pests and the sudden appearance of the beasts, leaving nothing to gather. As this repeated year after year, the lives of the people in the territory grew more desolate by the day. They were holding on only because of the abundant food stockpiled from the continuous good harvests of the past.

If this year's harvest was as poor as the last, it might be truly difficult to endure.

"Even if the imperial army protects the territory," Raul said with sincerity, reading his master's worries, "His Majesty the Emperor will demand an excessive price in return."

Claire took a steadying breath, her nerves fluttering as she stepped inside Cardein Castle. Her reddish-brown curly hair was tied up simply with a red ribbon that matched her dress. The garment flowed naturally from her shoulders, a departure from the wide, flared designs currently in fashion. Simple and unadorned, it was made stylish by a decorative piece at the waist. It was a design she had made and worn herself.

*I'm busy finishing Lady Catherine's dress, so you go see Lady Estella. You can make that dress under your name. Think of it as your debut.*

Madame Lucretia had said it as if she were granting a great favor, but Claire knew it was a calculated decision. Making a dress for Lady Estella was seen as a task that would only harm a designer's reputation, not help it.

Though it was her first time entering the castle alone, Claire was very excited.

*Finally, I can get rid of that bizarre dress!*

She, too, found it difficult to look at the gowns that so poorly suited Lady Estella's figure. They were said to be made according to the lady's own taste, but as a designer, Claire could never accept them. The image of Lady Estella in a frilly dress, smiling awkwardly, often floated before her eyes as she tried to sleep. *If it were me, I would never have made such a dress. No matter how much the lady insisted, I would have persuaded her somehow.*

Hoping that Lady Estella's taste had changed, even a little, Claire quickened her pace.

When she finally met Estella, however, she was startled by completely unexpected words.

"Pardon? You're not having a new one made?"

"No. I want you to reform the existing dress."

"Reform? I'm sorry, what's that?"

She had worked at the dress shop for nearly ten years but had never heard the word.

"Ah, it means to alter it," Estella explained. "Could you come this way?"

"Ah, I see. Reform!"

Claire quickly jotted down the new word in her notebook, determined to remember it.

-Reform, a term for altering an existing dress.

For Claire, who loved learning new things, this was an unexpected gain. Her heart pounded with a sudden feeling that this job would be more enjoyable than she had ever imagined.

*The lady seems different than before.*

As she followed Estella towards the dress room, Claire could not hide her puzzlement. She couldn't pinpoint exactly what had changed, but the air around the lady felt subtly different. Her shoulders, which had always been slumped, were now held confidently straight, and her steps were firm and unhesitating.

*Her way of speaking... has it changed?*

Lost in thought, Claire almost didn't realize they had arrived in front of the vast dress room.

"What do you think?" Estella asked, gesturing inside. "Of these dresses?"

"Ah..."

Claire was at a loss for words, struck dumb by the feast of technicolor spread before her eyes.
