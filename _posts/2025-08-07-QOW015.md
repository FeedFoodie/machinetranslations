In the three months since Yoon Seoyi had opened her eyes as Estella, her body had transformed. She was now half her former size, a change so astonishing that the marquis's servants stared with wide eyes. Her once obese figure was gone, leaving behind a frame that could now be described as merely chubby. It was no surprise, then, that none of her old dresses fit her anymore.

"Hasn't Lady Estella gotten so pretty lately?"

"I know, right! I think she's starting to look more and more like her late mother."

"And her whole aura has changed so much."

The change was reflected even in Estella's wardrobe. She now favored dresses of a simple design with minimal decoration, a stark contrast to the gowns plastered with tissue-like lace she once wore. Most were reformed versions of dresses Catherine had tailored for her, but she had sold off the garish yellow, orange, and green dresses for a pittance, unable to bring herself to wear the traffic-light-like collection.

"That's right. Lady Elina was such a beauty."

"She was no ordinary person, and her character was so admirable."

The maids who had served the marquis's estate since Estella's mother was alive remembered Elina clearly. She had been a kind mistress who never treated her servants poorly.

"She's even receiving swordsmanship training these days, you know."

"Right, right! I heard it when I was passing by the training grounds. The knights were calling our young lady a genius."

"A genius?"

If there was one word that seemed utterly unrelated to Estella, it was 'talent'. To hear her called a 'genius' was baffling, and it was impossible not to be skeptical.

"Yes! They said she's a swordsmanship genius, the kind that appears once in a hundred years. They said if you teach her one thing, she understands ten. I heard that even though she hasn't been learning for long, she's already good enough to hold her own against most of the knights."

"Nah, that can't be right. No way."

No matter how much Lady Estella had changed, calling her a swordsmanship genius felt like too much of a stretch.

"It's true, I tell you!" Frustrated by her colleague's disbelief, the maid who had relayed the story pounded her chest.

As Estella was the hottest topic in the marquis's household, any place the servants gathered was always abuzz with talk of her. The conversations, which had once been filled with slander, were now mainly praise for her changed appearance.

However, not all the chatter was in Estella's favor. A maid who had been listening to the praise for a while shook her head. She was Natalie, Catherine's personal maid.

"Still, Lady Catherine is the best. Didn't she give all the maids expensive hand cream Besides, two days ago, she went to the orphanage to volunteer, and is that all? When the monsters appeared in the nearby forest recently, Lady Catherine rushed to the temple and prayed from morning until late at night. I heard she didn't even take a sip of water until her prayers were finished. I think it's thanks to her prayers that the knights were able to defeat the monsters safely. The gods must have been moved. Our Lady Catherine is an angel sent from heaven."

The other maids who heard Natalie's words nodded in agreement.

"That's true. There's no one like Lady Catherine. Isn't our young lady a true saint?"

As the conversation that began with Estella ended with praise for her, Catherine, who was standing nearby, secretly eavesdropping, smiled in satisfaction.

Right beside her, Lucille let out a soft sigh, but Catherine, absorbed in the talk about herself, did not notice.

For Catherine, there was nothing money could not buy, and a person's heart was the easiest and most cost-effective purchase of all. 

Her volunteer work was no different. She had simply hired someone who looked similar to her, had their hair dyed the same color, and sent them to the temple with their face covered by a veil. The person who received the money was happy to earn it just by kneeling, and Catherine was happy to receive praise without wasting any of her own time. It was a situation where no one lost out. Thanks to her unusual hair color, most people assumed it was her as long as the hair matched.

Only Lucille knew about the existence of the body double. For the price of a few coins, Catherine became an angel and a saint blessed by the gods.

Her mood lifted, Catherine stepped out from her hiding spot, appearing before the two maids as if she had just arrived.

"Good morning."

"Good morning, my lady."

"My lady, thank you so much for the hand cream!"

The two maids spotted Catherine and scurried over with the brightest expressions, bowing deeply.

"It's nothing compared to all your hard work," Catherine said, smiling gently as she took their hands in hers. "I'm the one who should be thankful."

The two maids flinched in surprise and quickly pulled their hands away. 

"...?" Catherine's eyes widened as if asking what was wrong.

"My hands are dirty, my lady. I was just cleaning a moment ago!"

"I'm worried I'll get dirt on your hands, my lady."

The two of them looked truly flustered.

"Don't say that. You have no idea how grateful I am for you both."

"My lady..."

The maids' eyes welled up with tears. 

Catherine, who had been smiling gently at them, widened her eyes and asked, "By the way, is it true that my sister has talent for the sword?"

To anyone watching, she had the face of someone delighted by hearing someone praise her sister.

"Yes, my lady! I heard it myself. They say she learns incredibly fast." The maids, eager to please Catherine, quickly relayed what they had heard.

"Really? That's such a relief. I should go see my sister."

Catherine hurriedly bid the two farewell and left. Lucille, who had been standing silently by her side, quickly followed.

As soon as the maids were out of sight, Lucille took out a wet towel and offered it to Catherine. The handkerchief, scented with high-quality perfume oil, gave off a deep floral fragrance. 

Catherine irritably snatched it from Lucille and roughly scrubbed at the hands that had touched the maids. She then threw the used handkerchief on the floor, and Lucille quietly picked it up.

Catherine felt like she was going to die of irritation. It seemed as if everything she had meticulously built for so long was starting to go awry just as success was within reach. She was afraid that if she left things as they were, she would end up with nothing. It was frustrating enough that Estella kept losing weight no matter how many weight-gaining potions she was given. Moreover, Catherine could not shake the feeling that her sister was actively avoiding her.

"I have to go to the training grounds," she said, her pace quickening. "Go to the kitchen and bring the cookies and cake I mentioned yesterday."

The amount of snacks Lucille brought from the kitchen was so large it required five maids to carry. The sudden order had thrown the kitchen into chaos, forcing the staff to bake cookies and cakes all night. They endured the hardship, however, telling themselves it was for the knights who had worked so hard to subjugate the monsters. Furthermore, it was a request from the kind-hearted Lady Catherine, so they would have finished it all even if it meant staying up for two nights instead of one. That was the power Catherine's name held in the marquis's household.

Until now, that is.

Just as the last basket was loaded into the carriage, a familiar vehicle entered the marquis's estate.

"Was Dmitri supposed to come today?"

*To think I'd forgotten this day!*

Catherine had been so consumed by the sudden changes in Estella that the day had completely slipped her mind. 

Lucille, just as distracted, turned pale upon seeing the carriage and quickly bowed her head. "I'm sorry, my lady. It's my fault for not reminding you."

Catherine glared at her as if she could kill her, but her anger was cut short. Dmitri's carriage had already arrived in front of her.

The carriage door opened to reveal Dmitri. With light brown hair and eyes as blue as the sea, he was as beautiful as a sculptor's masterpiece. His broad shoulders and narrow waist made his clothes fit exceptionally well. The weary expression he wore vanished the moment he spotted Catherine, replaced by a bright smile that seemed warm enough to melt all the cookies and cake in the baskets.

"Dmitri."

"Catherine."

The two naturally embraced, then looked at each other and smiled fondly.

"It's the day you meet my sister, isn't it?" Catherine asked.

Dmitri's sweet smile vanished without a trace. "Ugh," he sighed, running a hand through his well-groomed hair. "I don't know how much longer I have to keep this up."

"Why do you say that? My sister likes you so much."

"You know better than anyone where my heart lies."

Dmitri felt a surge of frustration and anxiety. He was always this way with the ever-so-kind Catherine. Estella was a fiancée forced upon him by his family. She had been tolerable when they were young, but at some point, she had started gaining weight like a pig. Just imagining standing side-by-side with her at a wedding ceremony made his blood boil. If only Catherine were confirmed as the heir to the marquisate, his father would finally give up on Estella.

"Don't say that," Catherine said softly. "If my sister is sad, I'm sad too."

She smiled, though her jewel-like eyes welled with tears.
