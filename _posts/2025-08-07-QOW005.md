Most of the dresses were from Lily's Dress Shop, but every single one was so poorly designed it was painful to behold. The excessive frills, the voluminous puff sleeves that completely disregarded the wearer's body shape, and the endlessly flared skirts were particularly egregious. It was, in a word, a total disaster, crowned by the enormous ribbon attached to the back of the waist.

They were all her teacher's designs, however, so Claire could say nothing. She simply pressed her lips together, knowing a student could not carelessly disparage her teacher's work. Besides, Madame Lucretia's signature flamboyance was the most popular style among the local noble ladies.

"They're hideous, aren't they?" Estella's tone was quite harsh for someone who had worn them until now without a word of complaint. "Imagine how I feel wearing them."

While Claire stared with a blank expression, Estella strode into the dressing room. She pulled out a couple of the large, dark-colored dresses she owned and presented them to Claire.

"Take off all these frills and lace," Estella instructed, "and alter them into the simplest design possible. It won't take too long since you're not making them from scratch, right?"

"...Yes, but are you sure you don't want to have new ones made?"

Young noblewomen rarely wore the same dress more than once. The very nature of the fabric made washing difficult, so a slightly soiled dress was either thrown away or sold to merchants who dealt in second-hand clothing. The price for even a similar design could vary wildly depending on which noble lady had worn it, which was how uncommon it was for one to wear the same outfit multiple times.

Thinking she must have misheard, Claire asked again and again, but the answer was always the same.

"No. It's a waste to throw these away," Estella insisted. "For now, just take them and make them as simple as possible. It's a lean year, so we should save where we can. You're the expert, so you'll fix them to fit my body shape, won't you? I'll trust your judgment."

"If you could at least tell me what kind of mood you want for the dresses..."

"Hmm..." Estella considered for a moment. "Something that doesn't make me look like a pushover?" she replied with a bright smile. She had a round, cute face, but Claire felt a chill from her that she had never noticed before.

A week later, Claire returned to the marquis's estate with the diligently altered dresses. The madame scolded her, declaring it shabby for a noble lady not to have a new dress made, but Claire thought differently. She had worried the madame might fly into a rage about her altering the designs without permission. Fortunately, or perhaps unfortunately, she showed little interest in Lady Estella's dresses.

The fabric was truly luxurious, and Claire had worked on them for a week with barely any sleep. Just as the lady had requested, she had reduced the embellishments as much as possible, aiming for a design that would create a slimmer silhouette. Every snip of the scissors cutting away the lace felt incredibly refreshing, as if a long-held frustration was finally being released.

*If she just lost a little weight, the way she looks in a dress would change completely.*

*Don't you think putting a plain dress with few decorations on someone who already has little to show for herself will just make her look more pathetic? You still have a long way to go.*

That had been Madame Lucretia's assessment upon seeing the reformed dresses. Claire had worried she might interfere and demand the decorations be reattached, but her interest in Estella's dresses ended there. She was far too busy with the new dresses she was creating for Catherine to spare it another thought.

What a relief.

Claire had always been curious about the beauty hidden beneath Estella's formidable layers of flesh. If you looked closely, every one of her features was lovely, and her wavy blonde hair was luscious and fine. People who had seen Estella in her childhood would all say how beautiful she had been. That was years ago, however. Now, she was just a fat young lady, her past beauty seemingly erased.

Claire had tried her best with the alterations, but she worried about how effective they would be. She was also concerned that the simple design might actually emphasize Lady Estella's belly. That fear, however, turned out to be entirely needless.

"My lady... what on earth happened to you in the past week?"

Claire had thought her face looked a bit slimmer than before, but the dresses she brought were now unbelievably loose.

"What is she suddenly up to? Did she figure something out?" Catherine muttered irritably. She watched Estella, who was once again running around the training grounds this morning, drenched in sweat.

"Don't you think she'll quit soon?" Lucille replied in a casual tone, as if it were nothing. It was important to act as nonchalant as possible so as not to worry Catherine.

As the eldest daughter of Baron Delacroix, Lucille had been chosen from numerous candidates to become Catherine's handmaiden. Everyone in the territory knew that although Estella was the eldest daughter of Marquis Cardien, the real power lay not with the incompetent and unattractive first daughter, but with the second, Catherine.

Lucille did her best to win Catherine's trust. Becoming her confidante could guarantee a good marriage proposal, a position essential for her own social advancement. She was always careful not to get on Catherine's nerves and would put on all sorts of uncharacteristic displays of affection whenever Catherine seemed even slightly down. The weight-gain powder Catherine mixed into Estella's milk tea every morning had been procured by Lucille herself.

*Oh, Lucille! How thoughtful of you to find such a healthy supplement for my sister.*

Even as she accepted the powder, Catherine never once dirtied her own hands. She never directly referred to it as a weight-gain powder, either. She even praised Lucille in front of the marquis, saying, "Lucille found a special supplement for my sister's health." On the surface, it looked like a compliment, but her true intention was to shift all responsibility onto Lucille. If things went wrong, Catherine would be the kind, innocent lady who had been wrongfully used without knowing a thing.

"Cardien's Jewel."

Everyone called Catherine that, but she was a demon in an angel's mask. She was so meticulous that she never created a situation disadvantageous to herself and so adept at wearing her mask that she could even confuse her own attendants. In that respect, Lucille admired her. She was convinced that a person like Catherine was the one truly qualified to lead the marquis's family. Many of the marquis's vassals also believed Catherine would be the heir. The marquis did have a son with his third wife, but the boy was still young, and since the couple's relationship was poor, almost no one believed Luca would become the successor.

"Don't you think she's lost some weight?" Catherine's gaze was fixed on Estella, who had stopped running to gulp down water.

"She looks the same to me." Lucille could clearly see the difference, but she feigned ignorance to avoid upsetting Catherine.

"No. Something's changed. I'll have to find a way to deal with this."

Lucille sighed quietly to herself. When Catherine said she would "find a way," it meant that Lucille would have to handle all the troublesome work.

Estella gulped down three consecutive glasses of lemon water that Ria handed her, then flopped down in the shade of a tree near the training grounds. She had commissioned Claire to make her a set of workout clothes instead of the uncomfortable riding habit, which made moving around much easier.

"Ria, what about the thing I asked you to get?" Estella asked, gasping for breath as she wiped her sweat-drenched face with a handkerchief.

"I've found it."

Ria quietly held out a cloth bag. Stuffed with plants, the linen bag was puffed up like a blowfish. Estella grunted as she pushed herself into a sitting position. Her body was still so sluggish that even this simple movement required a great deal of effort.

"Oh! Give it here."

Estella's face lit up as she took the bag. She plucked off a leaf and began to chew on it.

"My lady! That hasn't been washed yet!" Ria shrieked in alarm.

Estella just waved her hand dismissively. She smacked her lips for a moment, as if savoring the taste, then broke into a wide smile. The flavor should have been bitter enough to make her tongue tingle, yet she didn't even frown.

"This is it. You did well."

It wasn't empty praise. The expression on Estella's face was brighter than ever.

Bileweed was a common weed that grew everywhere, thriving so well even in droughts that farmers were constantly busy pulling it out on sight. It had a minor, negligible effect of relieving fatigue, but its taste was so intensely bitter that it was difficult to use even for tea and was rarely treated as a medicinal herb.

"Dry this in the shade for at least a day," Estella instructed. "Then slowly burn it over a low flame, as if you're smoking it. A blue smoke will rise. Collect the ashes and bring them to me."

Ria listened with intense concentration, trying to memorize every single word. She had many questions, but she believed her lady had her reasons and that her duty was simply to obey.

"Yes, my lady. I'll do my best to make it."

"Thank you, Ria. I'm glad I have you."

At Estella's words, Ria's cheeks flushed with a shy blush. Just being by her lady's side was wonderful, but to run errands for her and even receive her thanks felt like a dream.

After several failed attempts, Ria finally obtained the ashes Estella wanted. She had stayed up all night, and when the blue smoke first rose, she was so happy that she let out a scream without realizing it. She didn't even feel tired. Clutching the jar full of ashes, her heart pounding, she stared endlessly out the window until dawn broke, then rushed to Estella's room.
