A dagger whistled past Denian, embedding itself in the tree right beside him with a force that startled a hiccup out of him. Denian, who would not even flinch at the sight of a monster, found Estella’s current action far more shocking. The nearby knights were just as stunned, their eyes wide as they stared at the dagger quivering in the wood.

"I can do it."

The surrounding knights held their breath, their attention fixed on the conversation between the two.

"You can't!"

SHIIING!

THWACK!

Another dagger slammed into the tree trunk, not even a finger’s width from the first.

VBRRRRR.

"......"

The new dagger quivered from the impact, and Denian hiccuped again, stealthily taking a few steps to the side.

Lucius’s eyes widened. *Since when did the young lady know how to do this?* He wracked his brain, but no one in the Marquis’s family had ever taught Lady Estella to use a dagger. *Did she learn on her own? But when?* Dozens of questions bloomed in his mind, but before he could grasp an answer, Estella’s voice cut through his thoughts.

"I just need to get Father’s permission, right?"

"......"

He opened his mouth to protest, to cut her off, but the words died the moment he met her resolute gaze. He saw then that her words were not born of some momentary whim. It was the same look he so often saw in the Marquis’s eyes, the eyes of his stubborn lord, a man who applied rigid standards to everything and possessed not an ounce of flexibility. A man who acted as if his only purpose in life was loyalty to the Emperor and the protection of his domain from monsters.

Lucius felt so helpless he could not think of a single thing to say. *Wouldn't it be better for her to just live lazily? What if she gets hurt, or even dies?* For a brief moment, an image of Estella’s kind and gentle mother surfaced in his mind. She had laughed so easily, cried just as easily, and found great joy in the smallest of things, but after giving birth to a daughter who looked just like her, she had fallen ill, closing her eyes forever before Estella was even a year old.

"Lucius," she said, her gaze firmer than ever before. "I have no intention of withering away in a vase like some rootless flower. I will stand on my own two feet, on my own land, and I will cultivate it myself. I want to protect the people of my domain with my own strength."

It was a battle he could not win. With a long sigh, Lucius slapped a palm to his forehead as if he had no other choice.

"Haa, I understand, but if the Marquis doesn't give his permission, it's absolutely impossible."

"Thank you. I'll do my best," Estella replied, as if she had already secured his approval. She smiled brightly then, wiping the blood from his forehead with her already grimy sleeve.

"Your face is in worse shape, my lady."

"It's fine. I'm young, so I'm pretty even with stuff on my face."

The strangely brave young lady had become shameless too. Lucius was certain of one thing. The Marquis, who loved his daughter more than anyone, would never permit her to do something so dangerous.

{sep}

"Let her do it."

That evening, however, things took a turn Lucius had not expected at all.

"......"

Lucius’s eyes went as wide as plums.

"It's been seven years."

"Pardon?"

"Since Estella has stepped forward to try something," the Marquis explained, his gaze unreadable as he looked at Lucius. "So, let's just wait and see for now."

"She could be seriously injured."

"That would be her burden to bear."

The Marquis was suspiciously calm, a stark contrast to Lucius’s anxiety.

"She could die."

He had not wanted to say it, but he had no other choice if he hoped to change the Marquis’s mind. The tactic seemed to work, for the Marquis flinched, but only for a moment.

"That, too."

"My lord!"

"Lucius, for some reason, I want to trust that child this time. So, you will watch over her from her side."

"I'm old, too."

Lucius tried a flimsy excuse, though even as he said it, he knew it was useless. He had, after all, handily dealt with a monster by himself just today, and there was no way the Marquis, who had already read the report, would not know that.

"I'm older, so you'll listen to me today."

"We're only two years apart," Lucius mumbled, not intending to be heard.

But the sharp-eared Marquis caught it precisely. "Two years and ten months. That's almost three years."

Lucius pouted again, but the Marquis pretended not to notice and turned his head. Cassian enjoyed these conversations with Lucius. Since there were few people with whom he could speak so openly, like a friend, it was a breath of fresh air that relieved his frustration.

"Teach that child swordsmanship. So she can at least protect herself if she meets a tough opponent."

"Can't you just assign a skilled knight to her so she doesn't get hurt?"

"A moment will come when no one is by her side."

How could the father and daughter be so equally stubborn? Lucius felt again that his words were having no effect. He had no choice but to threaten him in a different way.

"I will teach her harshly," Lucius declared, deliberately putting on a stern expression.

"Fine."

"I'll teach her until she's crying her eyes out. It'll be too late for regrets then."

"Fine."

The corners of the Marquis’s lips rose faintly. He could tell from Lucius’s grumbling tone that he was sulking. Watching him pout, so unbefitting of his large frame, brought back old memories.

"Do your subordinates know? That you're this petty?"

That was why Cassian, too, often found his old self emerging in front of Lucius.

Instead of answering, Lucius simply pouted once more with the lips hidden beneath his bushy beard.

The closer Estella got to the knights, the more anxious Catherine became.

"After everything I've done for them, they're siding with her just because she gave them a few lumps of iron?"

Lucille silently watched Catherine, who had been fuming and pacing the room all morning. She knew from experience not to approach Catherine when she was in such a tantrum, unable to contain her anger. It was not the first or second time she had suffered for it. Judging by her state now, getting away with just a slap to the cheek would be a good outcome.

*If it weren't for my marriage, I swear...*

She had volunteered for this position, beating out fierce competition on the promise that becoming Catherine’s confidante would guarantee a good marriage proposal. Her father, Baron Delacroix, had been overjoyed at the news.

*Our family's future depends on what you do. You must never do anything to fall out of Lady Catherine's favor.*

Like the other nobles of the domain, Baron Delacroix had no doubt that Catherine, not the eldest daughter Estella, would become the heir to the Marquis’s family.

"And why has she lost so much weight? Are you delivering the medicine properly? Are you putting it in her food correctly!" Catherine shrieked, her voice rising to a pitch that promised a hand might fly out at any moment.

"Yes, my lady. I've been mixing a little of the medicine into all the food Lady Estella eats."

"Then why is she like that? Why does she keep losing weight! Don't tell me the medicine's effect has worn off?"

"That can't be! I even tried it myself just in case," Lucille pleaded, her voice wronged.

Catherine knew this was true. Lucille was personally ingesting the medicine to prove its effects, and as a result, her once-slender jawline had become quite round. The effect was undeniable.

"Could it be because she's been exercising so much lately?" Lucille offered in a barely audible voice.

"Why does that pig-like bitch keep moving around and causing this mess? She should just stay cooped up in a corner of her room like before. So annoying."

Lucille felt a chill run down her spine as she watched Catherine spew such demonic words with her angelic face, but for the sake of a happy future, she had to endure the current hardship. Catherine, for all her wickedness, always kept her promises. Lucille had observed that much. In fact, since becoming Catherine’s handmaiden, the number of men who approached her at parties had increased significantly.

"Ah! That'll work!"

"Pardon?"

Catherine, who had been fuming, suddenly clapped her hands as if struck by a great idea.

"I just have to stop her from exercising. I just have to make it so she can't do anything."

"How..." Lucille asked, a sense of dread creeping into her voice.

"Do I have to tell you that, too?" Catherine asked, an angelic smile spreading across her face. "You should be able to figure that much out on your own. Right?"
