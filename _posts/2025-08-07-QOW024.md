Ciel caught his breath, his mind scrambling for a plausible excuse.

"That dagger," he said, the words rushing out. "It has the most incredible shape I've ever seen. Who on earth could have made something like this?"

It was a terrible excuse, and he knew it even as the words left his mouth. In truth, he had barely registered the dagger's shape at all. His gaze kept drifting back to Estella's flushed cheeks, and he could still feel the soft sensation where his fingertips had brushed her skin. The feeling was so pleasant that he found himself wanting to cup both her cheeks in his hands and knead them gently.

*He must be losing his mind.*

"You have a good eye!"

"Pardon?"

A jolt went through Ciel, who wondered for a moment if she had somehow read his thoughts. He soon realized, however, that his hastily concocted excuse had been quite fitting. Estella was showing him the dagger with an expression of pure confidence.

"A skilled mercenary like you has a discerning eye for weapons. It seems I'm a good judge of character."

Wearing a satisfied look, Estella held out the dagger she had been carrying.

"I'll give you special permission to examine it. You won't have seen anything like it anywhere else, and that's because I designed it myself."

Feeling bewildered, Ciel accepted the weapon. It was still warm from her grasp. He had planned to give it a quick glance and a casual compliment, but his opinion changed completely as he examined it more closely. Just as she had claimed, the dagger was perfect.

"It's light," he murmured, turning it over. "What are these serrations for? The center of gravity is perfect. It could pierce a target with absolute precision."

The dagger was neither heavy nor light. It lacked any common ornamentation, yet there was nothing to fault in the shape and size of the blade or the efficient design of its simple hilt.

Ciel was genuinely impressed. A sudden desire to own one for himself began to grow within him.

"However," Estella said with confidence, "the one being made now will be beyond comparison to this."

At her words, Ciel's gaze shifted to the closed door of the smithy, from which red and blue light pulsed in a steady rhythm.

The next day, as soon as the sun rose, Estella went to the blacksmith's forge with Ciel in tow.

Dolan looked utterly exhausted. He had clearly worked through the night to finish the daggers, which now lay completed on the table before him. He sat with his head bowed low, as if the weight of his fatigue was too much to bear.

"Dolan?" Estella called to him cautiously, wondering if he had fallen asleep. A belated sense of regret washed over her as she thought she might have pushed him too hard because of her own greed.

Dolan only lifted his head after she called his name a second time.

"What's wrong? What happened?" she asked, her voice rising in alarm. "Did you fail? It's okay! You don't have to cry so much over it!"

When Dolan finally looked up, his eyes were swollen like a goldfish's. The moment his gaze met Estella's, fat tears welled up and began to drop onto his grimy cheeks.

Estella hurriedly took out the handkerchief Ria had given her and began to wipe his tears away. She had wondered why she should carry such a thing, but it seemed it was for moments just like this.

Ciel watched her with a peculiar expression. She really was no ordinary noble lady. No noble lady in the world would use her own handkerchief to wipe the tears of a commoner, let alone an old blacksmith.

"It's alright!" Estella continued, spouting whatever came to mind in her confusion. "We can just make another one. You can take your time with it. I was too..."

"Thank you."

"What?"

Dolan's tearful words left her speechless.

*Suddenly? For what? Does he like being pushed? A workaholic?* All sorts of thoughts raced through Estella's mind in that brief moment.

"For giving me the chance... to create such a masterpiece... Sniffle, thank you so much, my lady!"

Dolan slowly rose from his chair only to drop to his knees on the floor, sobbing. His emotions were so intense that his nose started running. Feeling she could not possibly wipe his snot for him, Estella simply handed him the handkerchief.

A thrill that was hard to put into words coursed through Estella as she picked up a dagger from the table. The moment she did, she felt a sharp aura, so keen it tingled in her palm.

"It's a success."

The word 'overwhelmed' seemed to have been made for moments like this.

"Yes, my lady. Sniffle."

As he listened to their conversation, Ciel's gaze fell upon the dagger Estella was holding.

"!"

It was a blade that sent a shiver down his spine just by looking at it. Though it was only a dagger, it felt as if it could cut through anything in existence.

As if reading his thoughts, Estella handed him the dagger she had shown him yesterday.

"Hold this."

Ciel took it, wondering what she was about to do.

"......"

With a single, fluid motion, Estella sliced the Nebrion dagger through the blade Ciel was holding. It severed the steel as cleanly as a blade of grass.

"Th-this is..."

"What did I tell you?" Estella said with a smirk. "I said something even better would come out of it."

Ciel, completely dumbfounded, stared blankly at the severed blade.

*Who in the world was this woman? How did she know such things?*

The questions he already had about her multiplied uncontrollably, sprouting in his mind like mushrooms after a rain.

Gazing with satisfaction at Ciel's dazed expression, Estella took a step closer to him.

"For the next three years," she said. "If you become my exclusive mercenary, I'll supply your squirrels with weapons made from Nebrion. How about it? Tempting?"

Their previous contract was only until the end of the monster hunt, but last night, Estella had remembered that the Squirrel Mercenaries were the most skilled fighters in the empire. She knew they were framed and annihilated by the mad emperor, but she also knew that if she protected them, if she kept them from getting entangled in the incident that led to their false accusation, they would become the most reliable allies anyone could ask for.

"I'll discuss it with my men."

The words, *I'll agree if you let me touch your cheek just once,* almost slipped out, but Ciel barely managed to regain his senses. Three years was too long. He had never stayed in one place for so long after leaving the imperial palace.

"Alright. This is a bribe."

Estella handed him the Nebrion dagger she was holding and smiled brightly.

Ciel stared blankly at her face for a long moment before whipping his head away.

To think that even while holding such a masterpiece of a sword, a woman's cheek would be more captivating.

It was driving him absolutely crazy.

"I'm all for it!"

"My god! Just by mixing in useless Nebrion, a steel like this was created?"

"Did you see the whole process? How much Nebrion did they mix in? Was there a special method?"

As expected, his men practically foamed at the mouth, swarming the Nebrion dagger the moment they saw it.

"It's for three years," Ciel reminded them. "We need to think carefully before deciding."

"I'd do it for thirty years, not just three!" Riri shouted, holding the Nebrion dagger high like a torch. "If I can have a sword like this, I'd sell my soul!"

"Nobody wants your pathetic soul, Riri."

"Shut up!" Riri retorted harshly at Bruno's comment.

*Three years...*

Making a contract with Estella was not the issue. The problem was the duration. In times like this, it seemed best to ask for the opinion of the cool-headed Kyle.

"Where's Kyle?"

But Kyle, who had been admiring the dagger just moments ago, was nowhere to be seen. He had probably gone off to calmly organize his thoughts. Just as Ciel was thinking that Kyle was the only one he could truly rely on, a voice spoke up.

"There are no issues with the contract."

Kyle, having already finished reviewing the contract Estella had given them, stood before Ciel with a pen in hand.

"My Lord Marquis, Lady Estella requests an audience."

At the words of his butler, Raul, Cassian put down the document he had just started reading.

"I can't get any work done," Cassian muttered, feigning annoyance as he took off his glasses.

Raul, however, did not miss the subtle upward twitch of his master's lips. It seemed he pretended not to, but secretly enjoyed his daughters' visits. Raul quite liked this side of the marquis.

"Let her in."

As soon as permission was granted, Estella strode into the office. Even her stride was different from Catherine's.

Lately, Cassian found himself surprised by Estella's transformation every time he saw her. She seemed different again today, even from just the other day. Her face had grown a bit slimmer, her gaze had sharpened, and her steps were now firm and unhesitating. Judging by her aura alone, she was no less imposing than the captain of the knights.

"Father."

Estella smiled like the summer sun. Seeing his daughter's bright smile, which revealed her white, even teeth, Cassian found himself smiling back without realizing it. Her beautiful smile held a trace of Elina's face.

Estella smelled of sweat, so different from Catherine, who always carried a deep floral scent. The marquis let out another chuckle. It seemed there had not been a time recently when he had laughed so often because of his daughters.

"Yes, what is it?"

"Please take a look at this."

Estella abruptly placed something on the marquis's desk. She was just as she had been as a child, confidently presenting a difficult book she had managed to read. His eldest daughter did not offer empty pleasantries like Catherine, who would say she came because she missed her father's face or was just stopping by. A warmth entered the marquis's eyes as he looked at Estella, who had her mother's face but a personality that was the spitting image of his own.

What Estella had placed on the desk was a dagger he had never seen before.

The marquis realized at once that it was no ordinary object. The blade exuded an aura so sharp it made his fingertips tingle. This was not something made of common iron.
