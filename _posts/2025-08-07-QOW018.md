When the guard heard the name of Ciel's mercenary squad, his brow furrowed. "What did you say?" he asked again.

"The Squirrel Mercenaries."

"Pfft!"

The moment Ciel's confident reply left his lips, a snort of laughter escaped the guard at the castle entrance. He quickly tried to school his expression, likely thinking it rude, but he had to bite his lip hard to keep a grin from spreading across his face.

"I told you we should change the name," Bruno grumbled. "What kind of name is Squirrel?"

Ciel paid him no mind. What could he do? He was the type who did what he wanted, no matter what anyone said. Besides, he liked squirrels.

"What does the name matter?" Ciel countered. "Skill is what's important."

"T-That's true, but Squirrel... Hehehehehe."

In the end, one of the guards could not hold it in and let out a chuckle. It was understandable. Mercenaries usually chose intimidating names to match their image.

A sly smile played on Ciel's handsome face. "Because I like squirrels," he said. It was a playful smile, yet there was an air about him that prevented one from treating him lightly. The guard felt goosebumps rise on his arms without even realizing it.

"Please come in. How long do you plan to stay in Winterfall?"

"Well, I'm not sure," Ciel replied, thinking of the stories he had heard on his way. "I heard this town is crawling with them these days. It might take a while."

The guard nodded immediately. "That's right. It's why other mercenaries have been gathering here."

"Is it also true that whoever hunts a monster gets to keep it?"

"Of course! Just hunt them down for us. The Marquis will also grant a great reward!"

At the guard's refreshing answer, the faces of the Squirrel Mercenaries lit up. The Marquis had made the decision to eliminate the monsters as quickly as possible. Ever since a Grade 4 monster, the Goliath Hopper, had appeared in the Ruslin Forest, monsters began to show up frequently near the territory. Thankfully, they had only been Grade 4 and Grade 5 so far, but there was no telling what kind of creature might appear next.

A servant guided the valiant Squirrel Mercenaries to an annex on the west side of the Marquis's castle. The building was old and worn, but it was kept clean and seemed a comfortable place to stay.

"You can rest here today," the servant said, "and go to the Knights' barracks in the evening."

All mercenaries who entered the Marquis's castle were required to report to the Knights' barracks on their day of arrival. They had to provide details like their squad's name, number of members, and primary weapons to ensure they could be positioned appropriately for sudden deployments. The servant in charge of guiding them was very kind. His politeness, offered to a group of wandering mercenaries who were likely all the same, showed just how serious the Marquis was about monster extermination.

"Understood," Ciel said.

"By the way..." the servant, Tom, began, studying Ciel's face intently. "Have you perhaps been here before?" It was his first time greeting a mercenary squad, so there was no way he could have met the man, yet he could not shake the feeling that his face was familiar.

"This is my first time in Winterfall."

"I see."

"Perhaps it's because I have a common face."

"That can't be!" Tom waved his hands, vehemently denying Ciel's words. "You're strikingly handsome!"

Though his appearance was a mess from not having washed in a long time, Ciel was clearly no ordinary man. He had dark black hair with a bluish tint and mysterious green eyes that seemed to change color with the light. A strong jawline, a high-bridged nose, and lips more beautiful than a woman's completed a face that was by no means ordinary.

"Thank you for the compliment," Ciel said with a faint smile.

Tom stared blankly for a moment before quickly coming to his senses. "There's hot water and a change of clothes ready in the bathroom. After you've washed up, a meal will be prepared for you downstairs. The annex dining hall is on the first floor, to the far right."

At the news that they could finally wash, Riri let out a cheer.

"Yes! I thought I was going to die from wanting to wash so badly!"

"You don't die from not washing," Airi replied in a monotone voice.

Riri shook his head as if he was fed up. "We don't get along, we just don't. We're not compatible at all."

"Me neither," Airi added.

"PFFT! Serves you right."

Bruno snickered, seeing Riri get so helplessly owned by Airi. His timid laughter was cut short, however, by Riri's loud curses and flying kicks.

"Haven't you been hit enough? You won't come to your senses until your upper and lower lips are merged into one! When those thick lips of yours are mashed together, they'll look like monster dung. It'll be a sight to see!"

"Ugh! Ack! Aaargh! Hey!"

Bruno was too busy hopping around, dodging Riri's merciless assault. Even as the two of them caused a ruckus, the other four wore peaceful expressions, as if this was a daily occurrence. Tom, who had been watching with wide eyes, quickly bowed and excused himself when the bear-like Bruno started running in his direction.

"Well then, please rest comfortably!"

It seemed a very chaotic and strange mercenary squad had joined them.

After a refreshing wash and their first full meal in a while, their bodies felt languid. They were assigned four rooms in the annex. Riri and Airi shared one, while Har, who was quiet but talked in his sleep, shared another with Bruno, who snored loudly. Kyle, who had miraculously picked every last mushroom out of his meat stew, took a room for himself, as did Ciel. Ciel was the leader, and as for the fastidious Kyle, anyone sharing with him would have to endure his nagging until the moment they fell asleep.

Leaving his subordinates to their sweet, long-awaited rest, Ciel headed for the Knights' barracks.

He had stayed in the main castle a few years ago, when he had come here as an imperial prince. It had already been four years since he left the imperial palace, hating the power struggles. Some of his companions had been with him from the beginning, while others had joined along the way. Now the six of them, who entrusted their lives to one another, had become closer than family.

*As expected, a life of wandering suits my disposition perfectly.*

Thinking once again that he had done the right thing by leaving, he was walking toward the barracks when the sound of clashing blades cut through the darkness. KANG, KANG!

*Swordsmanship practice at this hour?*

It was deep in the night, when everyone should have been asleep.

Finding it strange, Ciel walked toward the training grounds. There, under the light of a single, hazy lantern, he discovered a plump woman swinging a sword. Her movements were astonishing. She stood alone, facing several iron rods that hung from a tree branch. As they spun like tops and rushed at her from different directions, she would swiftly change the angle of her sword to strike them away. It was too dark to see clearly, but there seemed to be at least five of them, and they were not made of wood. One wrong move could lead to injury.

KANG! KAKANG! KANG!

Each time the woman struck, the roped rods would swing like a trapeze, spin around, and rush at her again. They flew at a fierce speed, as if multiple enemies were attacking her without rest.

*To think you can practice like that!*

Ciel's interest was piqued by the training method. He himself was well-versed in swordsmanship, but he had never seen anyone practice like this. What an innovative technique!

KANG! KANG!

"......"

THUD!

The woman, who had been swinging her sword and changing directions in an instant, suddenly stopped and snatched an incoming iron rod out of the air.

"Who's there?"

At the same time, a rod she had not managed to strike spun around and flew toward her.

"Watch out!" Ciel shouted, startled, and shot forward to strike it down.

But the woman was faster.

TANG!

She lightly parried the rod coming from behind her, then caught another one flying toward her, stopping its movement completely. The distance between them was now no more than three or four steps.

Her forehead, beaded with sweat, was round. In fact, everything about her seemed round. Her chubby cheeks were flushed from the strenuous movement, her eyes were wide with caution at the stranger, and even the hair coiled into a bun atop her head was perfectly round. For a swordswoman, her body looked sluggish, which made Ciel curious about her identity.

"Who are you?" the woman asked, seeming just as curious. She wiped her round chin with the back of her hand, her breaths coming in quick, short gasps. She wore strangely designed clothes, and even under the dim light, the fabric looked to be of good quality.

"A mercenary?" she asked, making an assumption when Ciel did not answer right away. Anyone could tell from her clothes and concise speech that she was a person of high status.

"Yes, I arrived in Winterfall today. I am Ciel, the leader of the Squirrel Mercenaries."

"How many are there?"

Most people burst out laughing as soon as they heard the squad's name, but she seemed more curious about other things.

"Six of us."

"That's a small number for a mercenary squad," she observed. "You must be skilled."

Compared to the mercenaries who moved in hordes, it was an absurdly small number.

"Yes, we're not bad," Ciel replied. "May I ask who you are?"

He was curious who on earth she was, practicing her swordsmanship alone in the empty training grounds in the middle of the night.
